# Dumb Decisions That Worked

*A hall of fame. For chaos that somehow paid off.*

## The Hall of Fame

---

### 1. "This Jar of Spiders is Legally Part of My Body"
**Who:** Inquizo
**Session:** Referenced throughout; the roll is Session 1–2 era
**The Decision:** Inquizo had a jar of sharp spiders. The DM questioned whether it was a valid item or action. Inquizo argued — with complete sincerity — that the jar of spiders was legally part of his body and therefore not subject to normal item rules.
**Why It Was Dumb:** He is a kobold. He was arguing jurisprudence with the DM. The spiders are in a jar.
**What Actually Happened:** He rolled a 24 on Persuasion. The DM ruled in his favor. The jar of spiders was legally part of his body. This remains the highest confirmed Persuasion roll in the campaign.
**Verdict: Worked.** Catastrophically, beautifully worked.

### 2. Pressing Every Button on the Submarine
**Who:** Inquizo
**Session:** Session 2
**The Decision:** Immediately upon boarding a pirate submarine — a vessel he had never seen, operated by people who wanted to kill him, full of controls labeled for emergencies — Inquizo began pressing every button on the panel at random.
**Why It Was Dumb:** Water poured from the ceiling. Grungrak nearly fell through the floor hatch into the ocean. Reficul had to become a bull shark and was then locked outside the submarine in shark form. Magical darkness. The merman was watching.
**What Actually Happened:** Button 61 unlocked the gear room. The gear room had flight suits, dive equipment, and a pirate hat. Grungrak received the pirate hat as a gift. His smile was described as "the most pure smile." He had a better time than anyone.
**Verdict: Worked** (for Grungrak specifically; everyone else had a terrible time).

### 3. "See You in Hell" — The Self-Stabbing
**Who:** Reficul
**Session:** Session 3
**The Decision:** Mid-combat, in the middle of the throne room fight against Queen Ariel, Reficul stabbed himself. He said "see you in hell" and died.
**Why It Was Dumb:** He stabbed himself. In a fight he was actively participating in. On purpose.
**What Actually Happened:** He went to hell (confirmed: no death saves, direct transit). He came back — as he always comes back. The self-inflicted wound wasn't even ruled as cause of death; the medicine check of 21 couldn't explain it. He was also briefly revealed in his true changeling form: three feet tall, fiendish horns, white hair, blood that shifts between green and blue. He has since died and returned multiple times via this mechanic. The devil's deal continues to pay dividends. He is, by some metrics, currently unkillable.
**Verdict: Worked.** (Depending on your definition of "worked" and your comfort with "went to hell.")

### 4. Face-Hugging Mira to Block a Charm Spell
**Who:** Inquizo
**Session:** Session 3
**The Decision:** Queen Ariel hit most of the party with a magical charm via singing, forcing them to move toward her. Inquizo's improvised counter: physically attach himself to Mira's face to prevent her from seeing anything and therefore using visual spell components.
**Why It Was Dumb:** This is not a listed spell. This is not a mechanical action. A kobold suction-cupped to a person's face is not a recognized defensive strategy.
**What Actually Happened:** The DM ruled it worked "after a fashion." Mira was partially disrupted. The party survived the encounter.
**Verdict: Worked** (after a fashion).

### 5. Pressing Button 61
**Who:** Inquizo
**Session:** Session 2
**The Decision:** After flooding the submarine, opening the floor hatch onto the ocean, and losing Reficul overboard, Inquizo pressed button 61.
**Why It Was Dumb:** He had just pressed several buttons and received water from the ceiling, a near-drowning, and a bull shark situation. A reasonable person stops pressing buttons. He pressed button 61.
**What Actually Happened:** It opened the back room. The back room had the best stuff. Pirate outfits. Dive gear. The DO NOT TOUCH hat (which remains unpressed, a tragedy for completionists). Grungrak got a hat.
**Verdict: Worked.** The single most productive button press in campaign history.

### 6. Mira Tackling Reficul the Moment She Saw Him in the Fields of Asphodel
**Who:** Mira
**Session:** Session 5–6 (the Fields of Asphodel / underworld sequence)
**The Decision:** Upon seeing Reficul in the underworld — a context where he had recently died and she had reason to be concerned — Mira tackled him.
**Why It Was Dumb:** She is chronically ill with a strength modifier. He is a changeling with fiendish blood who has been to hell and back. The fields of the dead are not a tackle-friendly environment. There was no tactical reason for this.
**What Actually Happened:** It worked. Reficul was present and solid and real. The tackle confirmed it. The moment was one of the campaign's most chaotic expressions of affection.
**Verdict: Worked.** Also confirmed: Mira will tackle you if she's worried about you.

### 7. Mimicking Pmurt's Voice to Commandeer the Airship AI
**Who:** Inquizo
**Session:** Session 13
**The Decision:** After accidentally activating the airship's navigation AI by bumping a button while wearing Pmurt's stolen crown, Inquizo used his voice mimic ability to imitate Pmurt's voice and issue commands.
**Why It Was Dumb:** He had bumped the button by mistake. He was wearing a crown roughly as tall as he is. He has never piloted an airship. He did not know where Sypium was. He told the ship to go there anyway.
**What Actually Happened:** The AI accepted his voice authentication. The ship set course for Sypium — the party's next destination. The ship is now flying north. The entire second half of Session 13 happened because Inquizo bumped a button and then leaned into it.
**Verdict: Worked.** Accidentally, completely, and irreversibly worked.

### 8. Releasing Starglass Despite Protests
**Who:** Inquizo
**Session:** Session 13
**The Decision:** There was a glass case in the cargo hold. It contained a fleshy-robotic entity connected to the ship by breathing tubes. The party was uncertain about opening it. Inquizo pressed the release button despite this uncertainty.
**Why It Was Dumb:** The party had just watched a ship reboot itself mid-flight. They knew nothing about what was in the case. "Don't press the button" is generally good advice when something is connected to your only means of air travel via biological pipes.
**What Actually Happened:** Starglass was released. She revealed: the rune alphabet from Sessions 8–10 is her mother's language; the doctor who modified her also spoke to Numgis; her brother is protecting the glowing prophetess; and the danger is north, which is where they're going. She is the ship's biological heart and agreed to help them reach Sypium. She also said "He will hurt you. Don't." which is the most useful ominous warning the party has received in thirteen sessions.
**Verdict: Worked.** Pivotal lore. Possibly campaign-changing.

### 9. Grungrak Eating the Pomegranate Seeds
**Who:** Grungrak (George)
**Session:** Session 5–6 (Persephone's forest / underworld sequence)
**The Decision:** In Persephone's forest — which exists in or near the underworld, where eating food has documented mythological consequences — Grungrak ate pomegranate seeds. More than six. The DM noted the exact count. Grungrak did not know the mythology. He just wanted the seeds.
**Why It Was Dumb:** In Greek tradition, eating pomegranate seeds in the underworld binds you there. Persephone ate six seeds and is contractually required to return every year. George ate more than six. He is not from this world's mythology and did not know this. He found out later and has not fully processed it.
**What Actually Happened:** Nothing visible yet. The DM knows the count. The campaign knows the count. This is the kind of decision whose consequences arrive slowly and then all at once.
**Verdict: Pending.** Ask again in several sessions. The seeds are doing something. We just don't know what.

### 10. "He's Back, Guys!" — Announcing the Doctor to His Own Soldiers
**Who:** George (Grungrak)
**Session:** Session 14
**The Decision:** When Lieutenant Galveston arrived at Bermuda Foundation with soldiers — clearly working for the Doctor, clearly threatening — George broke the tension by announcing "He's back, guys!" as if they were all on the same team and he was just delivering good news.
**Why It Was Dumb:** Galveston had smoke gauntlets and was flanked by hellhounds. The party did not work for the Doctor. George was, at this exact moment, trespassing on the Doctor's burning property having arrived on the Doctor's stolen airship.
**What Actually Happened:** It bought the party precious seconds before combat started. The bluff didn't hold — Galveston was not fooled — but the momentary confusion (combined with George's total sincerity) gave the party time to position. The line is now one of the most quoted things George has ever said.
**Verdict: Worked** (briefly, chaotically, at maximum confidence).

### 11. "And Now" — The Velocity Lever
**Who:** Inquizo (directed by Halcyra)
**Session:** Session 14
**The Decision:** While trying to figure out the ship's velocity controls, Halcyra told Inquizo exactly when to release a lever. The plan was essentially: "Inquizo. Now." Neither of them fully knew what they were doing.
**Why It Was Dumb:** There was no instruction for this. The manual did not say "release this specific lever at this specific moment." The entire strategy was one person pointing at another person and saying go.
**What Actually Happened:** Because of the timing, Inquizo's luck, and George's morale, a hidden wall panel flipped open. It said "Activate velocity." That was the thing they needed. The DM described it as "sheer dumb fucking luck in Quizio."
**Verdict: Worked.** The panel opened. The ship got faster. Nobody questioned it.

### 12. Grabbing the Clock Hands
**Who:** Inquizo
**Session:** Session 14
**The Decision:** The grandfather clock was spinning wildly, the room was actively rusting, and Starglass was hyperventilating. Inquizo decided to grab the spinning clock hands and hold them still. His reasoning, stated out loud: *"because it was stupid."*
**Why It Was Dumb:** He grabbed a spinning clock. That's it. That was the plan.
**What Actually Happened:** The hands stopped. The rusting slowed. Starglass's breathing steadied. Gbot'n deployed dozens of mage hands to help hold them in place. The session ended with Inquizo and a pug holding a clock together in a rusting cargo hold as the session ended.
**Verdict: Worked.** Sometimes "this is stupid" is all the justification you need.

**Inquizo grabbing the mine clock's hour hand (Session 15)** — The clock was spinning. The hands were dangerous (one of the Aspens grabbed the spinning hands and was flung off a cliff). Inquizo flew over, grabbed the hour hand, and was spun in circles. Repeatedly. What he had going for him was body weight and stubbornness. He didn't stop the clock on his own, but his braking combined with Mira's Mage Hand and two docile skeletons stopped it together. He was spun in circles for the duration. He did not let go. The clock stopped. **Verdict: Worked.** Unheroic persistence is still persistence.

### 13. Throwing the Clock Off the Bridge
**Who:** Inquizo
**Session:** Session 16
**The Decision:** There was a glowing blue clock bolted to the underside of a bridge over a very long drop. Inquizo climbed out onto the railing, balanced on it (acrobatics 21), and threw the clock off. He had already been told that breaking these clocks makes things worse. He had already watched what the Starglass clock did. He threw it anyway.
**Why It Was Dumb:** Every clock the party has encountered has caused reality-warping consequences when tampered with. One reversed time. One created a time loop. One distorted relative time. The pattern was clear. Inquizo climbed out onto a bridge railing over nothing and threw the clock into the void.
**What Actually Happened:** The sky went red. The sun disappeared. A stained glass dragon burst through the tunnel wall and took 9 damage points off Inquizo. The two prophecy stanzas that had been lingering as "metaphor" — *dragon scale shining in your eye* and *the sky's untrue* — came true simultaneously. The party survived. The sky came back. The train pulled into Alastair. Nobody acknowledged how insane that was.
**Verdict: Worked.** Technically. Everyone is alive and they're in the capital. Whether "turned the sky red and triggered two prophecy events" counts as "working" depends on your definition.

### 14. "CANNONBALL" — Sliding Through a Goop Monster
**Who:** George (Grungrak)
**Session:** Session 17
**The Decision:** A horned, winged goop-figure threw a glob of slime onto the stairs beneath George. Instead of dodging, George threw himself down the stairs feet-first, screamed "CANNONBALL," and aimed himself directly at — and through — the goop monster itself.
**Why It Was Dumb:** He launched his own body at a hostile creature made of goop, on purpose, while already sliding downhill on a surface that same creature had just made slippery. There was no plan beyond the word "cannonball."
**What Actually Happened:** Acrobatics 19. George slid clean through the goop-figure and came out the other side completely coated in slime — "Nickelodeon slime," by his own description. His verdict: *"This is gross. I need a shower."* The goop-figure's taunt ("Afraid of getting a little wet?") went unanswered, because George was already past it.
**Verdict: Worked.** Filthy, but worked.

## Honorable Mentions

**Inquizo going invisible the moment serious conversation started (Session 8)** — Not a dumb decision, just extremely on-brand. Rolled Stealth 16. Nobody noticed until he was long gone. Consequence: none. Grade: A perfect crime.

**Reficul licking Goo off an eel (Session 7)** — He rolled Sleight of Hand 18 to smear his own goo on a dry eel during a fight. The goo reacted with a spell and spawned a CR3 mummy on a tentacle. This was not a good plan. The mummy hurt people. It is on the list because it took a specific kind of commitment to use your own body fluid as a tactical weapon and then accidentally summon something.

**Halcyra catching Naamon after Reficul punched him off the platform (Session 13)** — Not dumb, just extremely hopeful. She caught him. He died anyway from the punch. Verdict: a noble failure. Respectfully excluded from the main list.
