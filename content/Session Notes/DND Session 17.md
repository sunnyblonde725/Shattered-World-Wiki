# DND Session 17 — Encounter with the Watchers
*The Watchers' Web*

---

## TL;DR

Picking up from last session's cliffhanger, the party rescues the Prophetess from a melting-skinned boy, meets a friendly hammer-wielding bug named Haki, and gondolas across to a 300-foot clock tower where cultists are being drained to power a sentient Machine. The Machine shows each of them visions of who they were — or could be. Halcyra sees a blue dragon, laurels, and her mother in a burning city. Mira touches the Machine and gets the single biggest vision of the night: a giant scythe, a goddess turned to stone, and a third voice that isn't the Machine or the Prophetess. George says no to his vision and gets hit with lightning for it anyway. Halcyra's Divine Smite (17 to hit) destroys the Machine entirely — and wakes something much worse underneath, a wyrmling-thing "made to halt progress." The party flees through a glass door into a cosmic library, where a god named Oravis (the God of Stories) tells Halcyra that her father died to bring her back to life, that her mother is out searching for her on an airship called The Eagle, and that her own prophecy scroll has a line that's been about her the whole time. Mira learns her power comes from the Ancient God of Chaos. Liz — Constance — pulls a gun, says "yeah," and vanishes into shadow with a mysterious figure, leaving the party for good. And then they crawl into an actual spider's web, where a second Watcher — an enormous spider-woman — puts Inquizo to sleep for the rest of the session, tells Halcyra about a "contract" she doesn't remember, and hands Mira four new lines of prophecy with absolutely no explanation of what they mean.

## Full Summary

### Scene 1 — The Boy on the Platform (00:00–42:51)

Picking up directly from Session 16's cliffhanger: the burnt-skinned, melting boy (roughly 18 years old) lunges at the Prophetess, snarling **"You will not take her from me."** Inquizo grapples and hog-ties him — a combined roll of 22 (17+5) gets the job done.

With the boy restrained, Halcyra approaches the Prophetess (who appears to be around 17.5 years old) directly. The Prophetess confirms she's from a different world, that **Theta is dead**, and gives Halcyra a vision: **Numgis, standing at a clock tower, beside a mechanical figure and a broken, wildly spinning grandfather clock.**

Meanwhile, Mira and Lila face down a massive bug — **Haki** — who turns out to be friendly. He's a hammer-wielder with "a score to settle at the clock tower," and he's too big to fit inside the gondola, so he rides on top of it instead.

A new figure joins the party at this point: a **bald woman with bioluminescent eyes and a sword**, unnamed for now. The party — plus the bound boy, Haki riding on top, and the new woman — boards a gondola heading toward **the palace and the clock tower.**

---

### Scene 2 — The Gondola, Millie, and the Courtyard (42:42–1:25:15)

The bald woman introduces herself as **Millie**. Partway across, she deploys a **mind-control bug** — the same species as the one from "Canaries' Gentle Descent" (Session 15, the Stuart connection) — onto the bound boy's head. **Mira cuts the elastic mind-control thread with her sword** (a Strength check of 17), and the boy goes unconscious.

The Prophetess heals the boy, then — before anyone realizes she has the ability to do it — **grants the entire party Heroic Inspiration**. She departs with Millie, who carries off both the unconscious boy and a separate unconscious figure in **steampunk "iron man" armor named Gabaton**.

Millie, while charging George, rolls a **critical miss (nat 1 + 10 = 11)**. Halcyra's **Persuasion 14** gets Millie's name out of her. George's **History 16** connects Millie to the ship **Starglass** ("Star Glass"). Millie reveals she's fleeing **"the Doctor"** (Dr. Larkin) and is heading to the mountains via someone/something called **"Scipio"** — she expects to cross paths with the party again later, at the ship Starglass.

The gondola reaches a **mountain station** — a torn-open gateway, blood on the ground — and the **300-foot clock tower** and its courtyard come into view.

In the courtyard: rows of kneeling **Dreamcatcher Squadron cultists** are having their energy drained directly into the clock tower by a tusked figure, **Dervnumgus**. This is **the same clock tower from the Prophetess's earlier vision** — except now its hands have **stopped**, where before they were spinning wildly. **Gore Harlow** stands alongside Dervnumgus.

Inquizo's first Invisibility check rolls a **4** and fails — this establishes a new houserule on the spot: **a natural 1 on an invisibility-type roll means you believe you're invisible when you are not.** He then rolls **Stealth 24** and sneaks past a garden of hollow, warm "Mert" statues (marble, quartz, ivory) to reach the tower. A **41% eavesdrop roll** picks up part of Dervnumgus and Gore Harlow's conversation: the clock tower is referred to as **"a woman"** who is **"almost reaching my climax,"** and Dervnumgus promises Gore Harlow **"the fortune"** and **"control of your home"** once "all of this is over" — the conversation is cut off before more details emerge.

Inside the tower, tendrils with strange blue-and-yellow blocky faces can't see Inquizo. **THE MACHINE makes telepathic contact with Inquizo alone**: *"Welcome to... I can give you what you have always wanted... a second chance... Come feed the machine."* Three gobo-light images play in sequence: a burning dragon/demon city, an island where angelic beings carry away much larger people, and "a real face." The Machine extends a glass hand toward him; Inquizo backs off 20 feet.

Halcyra rolls a 2 (rerolled to 3) on a check near a **"pulsing energy"** and receives **"a vestige"** — what this means is unresolved. A **Thorin statue** is also present in the tower, and Halcyra recognizes it.

### Scene 3 — The Machine's Visions (1:25:17–2:11:24)

Inside the clock tower proper, a "glass menagerie" of figures kneels as the party enters. Halcyra's **Religion 14** identifies something as **"primordial"** (a second, unconfirmed Religion check around 24 also happens here).

Above the Machine, three floating, ink-black "stars" with crimson eyes hover roughly 100 feet apart inside the 300-foot tower, connected by spooky, tendril-like "Tenebrous" goop. **THE VOICE** — the Machine — addresses the whole party at once, offering to show each of them who they were, or who they could be, in exchange for "time."

**To George**, it offers to show him who he was. He declines — *"right now it's not that time"* — and the Voice responds, *"Alright... see me in [some]%... this is the case."*

**To Halcyra alone**, four stained-glass visions appear:
1. Halcyra, a **blue dragon**, and a hammer ripping apart the sky
2. A **laurel wreath** (likely Apollo-coded)
3. Halcyra alone, surrounded by laurels
4. The city of **Erlon** on fire, full of demons and dragons — with **Halcyra's mother** visible inside it (the DM clarifies Erlon is NOT the same place as Aerolond)

Halcyra touches the laurel image and it spins. She asks what the visions mean — *"Possibilities. Everything can be changed. Everyone can be given a second chance."* She asks what she'd remember if she touched the Machine directly — *"You'll remember everything and every possibility."* **She does not touch it.** The light fades once George has declined, visible to the whole party.

Mira is asked if she's had a vision yet: **"Not yet."**

(Comic aside: Inquizo successfully lures away one of the Machine's slower glass hands.)

Later, once combat has begun, **Mira touches the Machine** — declining Halcyra's offer to help ("way too focused") — and strikes it, "shattering" it, though she can't hit all three eyes at once. This triggers the **biggest shared vision of the night**, seen by the entire party:

Mira is holding a **giant scythe**, placed into her hands by a towering **statue of a woman** ("**the Lady**" — long silver-and-black hair, covered in flowers and vines). This is confirmed to be **the same bloodthirsty scythe George/Grungrak once wielded "from this very spot."** The vision runs in reverse: the Lady and a small boy — who, full-grown, would be roughly 18 feet tall, implying a giant (likely **Argus**) — walk backward into boats. Mira finds she can no longer understand their language. She feels warmth radiating from the **wheat seeds from Asphodel** (given to her by Reficul, in a past session — he does not appear this session).

The vision continues to reverse: the Lady waves her hand and seeded fields grow instantly across 10 feet of ground. Then it plays forward as a time-lapse: people build homes; Argus grows older; **Naf** and a group of "fanged beasts" live peacefully alongside them; then **winged mechanical beings "a bit like Icarus,"** armed with hammers and nails, attack. Argus defends the Lady, takes nails meant for her, and throws them back. The mechanical beings abduct people. The Lady gathers survivors into a temple — and the nails turn her, and others, **to stone**. She becomes the statue. Before she "passes," she gives Argus the scythe. He fights the mechanical beings, **cleaves one clean in half**, and leads survivors to caves where flying boats evacuate everyone. He fails to revive the Lady. Finally, he **"looks through" Mira** directly.

**Time stops.** A **"secret third" voice** — not the Machine, not the Prophetess, the same voice that previously told Mira to "kill something in the underworld" — speaks: *"It's a pity how things have to end." / "This is what it perpetuates." / "Good luck."* Mira asks aloud whether this is the same voice as before. It's left unanswered.

Combat is woven through all of this. The tendrils form a spider-web net; the Voice says, *"Oh good. You're mine now,"* directed at Mira. Mira **Dodges with advantage plus a Bardic Inspiration die from the Prophetess** (which she didn't know she had) — an effective natural 20 — but still takes 5–7 splashes of ink.

**Inquizo's "white flash" attack** lands two hits that slice through **every tendril at once**, plus a **bonus +8 lightning damage** from a homebrew item (usable a number of times per long rest equal to proficiency bonus, per Ian's ruling) — accompanied by a dragon-shaped visual and a lightning effect. Immediately after, Inquizo is hit with **"brain fog"** — the smell of maple and oil, the sound of dripping — and feels compelled **to kill the second eye**, climbing the railing toward it (roughly 200 feet up). A Constitution check is called for; the result is unclear.

A goop puddle on the ground rises up — briefly taking "nearly the form of Gore Harlow" before resolving into a **completely new figure**: a long trench coat, regency-era clothing, long horns, tiefling-like wings, dripping in goop. It taunts, *"Afraid of getting a little wet?"* and throws a goop "snowball" — it misses the airborne Halcyra and lands near George on the stairs, accelerating his slide. George shouts **"cannonball,"** rolls **Acrobatics 19**, and slides straight through the goo-figure, ending up completely covered ("Nickelodeon slime"): *"This is gross. I need a shower."*

### Scene 4 — The Machine Falls (2:11:24–2:53:11)

George lands a second, deeper hit on the Machine with melee plus **Thunder Wave** (confirmed Thunder, not Lightning).

**Mira and George share a private vision** — no one else sees it. A version of Mira roughly 10x normal size approaches. Three voices argue: *"Get out of my head," "Please just come home,"* and *"All of you will die soon. But I have a new little visitor."* The entity describes this as a picture from **"just before a Cataclysm"** and asks if they'd like to change it. George's player brings up **"Grungrake"** — a past self/separate figure who did NOT regret his choice. He "saved who was most important to him" (himself) and **did not survive** — something George has apparently been told before, "several times, in a river... in a shadow." The entity dismisses them both: *"You're happy with the time you've spent... You're of no use to me."* As they're ejected from the vision, **both Mira and George take 12 lightning damage** (a max roll) — George: *"Oh, it's lightning! No!"*

**Iskander, Liz, and an automaton emerge from the Machine — sharing a single body.** Liz and Iskander are confirmed to be **adopted siblings** ("yes, no, sort of"). The automaton wears a man's mask, **cleaved down the middle like a cross**, and reaches toward the Machine.

**Halcyra attacks the Machine itself (not the eyes) with Divine Smite, rolling a 17 to hit.** The Machine is destroyed. Every tendril is severed. The merged beings are split apart. The automaton goes inert — "rigor mortis." Iskander clutches his chest and head. Liz holds her arm. The Machine collapses and flattens into a circular glass **"hobbit-style door."**

A small **wyrmling-shaped entity** rises from the wreckage and pleads: *"I am [good?]. Why destroy my eternity when all that awaits you... is nothing but air and mar and cataclysm, over and over again? I would be the moment that stops you from your pain... from losing everything."* George: *"I've done that once, I've recovered."* / *"Ayo!"* The entity: *"You cannot decide my fate."* Halcyra: *"Nor can you decide ours. Ayo!"* The entity: **"I was made with one purpose — to halt progress."** Asked who made it, it answers simply: **"My father."** It refuses to discuss "semantics" further. The party rejects it outright — and it **grows larger than the clock tower itself**, the building around it rusting and growing vines, a gaping maw opening directly above. The party flees toward the Machine's glass door.

**Rescue attempt for Iskander:** Halcyra's **Athletics 18** gets her to Liz and Iskander. A **shadowy figure** — referred to as "the standard" or **"Dyrkonogus"** — appears within the ink and grabs Halcyra: *"Let go."* This figure has appeared to Halcyra before, "in this void." Halcyra, emotional: *"I haven't even seen him for so long... I can't leave him. He's mine."* A contested roll (with Bardic Inspiration, advantage granted by the DM, d6+d8) lands "at par with 19" — needing exactly 19 — and a **coin flip comes up tails: she fails.** ("It's fate.") The DM gives Halcyra a choice: let Liz go, or keep her. Halcyra looks at Liz and asks, *"Do you want to go with them?"* **Liz pulls out a gun, says "Yeah,"** and disappears into the shadows with the figure. **Liz/Constance has left the party.** Iskander's exact fate is unclear — he's likely taken as well [NEEDS CONFIRMATION].

The final escape requires Athletics 13+; Mira rolls **21** ("not saving a guy"), and both she and Halcyra make it out. On the way, the party glimpses a ruined city below — clear blue skies, but **green, old, rusty, bronze-and-copper ruins**, flying flags that are identified as belonging to **"the pirates that you once helped"** [NEEDS CONFIRMATION which faction].

The party tumbles through the Machine's floor-set glass door into a **massive library** — its ceiling is a night sky, and the space feels "peaceful." **George gains a level of exhaustion.** A kindly bearded man in glasses offers Mira and Halcyra magic tablets that restore health and spell slots ("the script says you eat it"). He introduces himself: **ORAVIS, THE GOD OF STORIES.** He's openly unhappy about "your numbness"/Numgis — *"a villain in my story... I needed."* Halcyra: *"We're not the biggest fan of him either. When will we get rid of him?"* Oravis: *"that's my friend's job... I think you'll meet her soon"* (likely the Prophetess). He references a book, **"the guillotine and the thought-turning realm,"** which Inquizo recognizes and loves. **Inquizo's Religion 16** recognizes Oravis and/or a figure called **"AGHAAD"** — recalling a statue of Aghaad that appeared "on the train" in a previous session [NEEDS CONFIRMATION]. A Deception/Insight check confirms Oravis is "very earnest." He pulls out "the story of Apollo driving his chariot" — and also **THE BIBLE**, which he throws away: *"Boy, have mankind ruined this."* Halcyra: *"Tell me about it."*

Then, to Inquizo: *"Hi, Inquisio, should I tell them your brother's name?"* Inquizo, alarmed: *"Don't you know who that is?... I feel very conflicted right now."* The name is **not** revealed — **Inquizo has a brother, and Oravis knows his name.** Halcyra asks about George's "story"/birthdate; Oravis answers, *"it's a Tuesday"* — possibly hinting at a shared birthday with another party member [NEEDS CONFIRMATION]. Finally, Oravis says he has "one question" for **Mira**, about "part one" of "a pamphlet" — unresolved, continuing into the next scene.

### Scene 5 — The Watcher's Library (2:53:11–3:29:58)

This is one long, continuous scene. A roughly 600-page book — the party's own chronicle — ends with the line: **"And the miracle child died."**

Oravis identifies himself fully: **"I am what one would call a watcher. We are an ancient group of gods."** He names the other domains: Stories (himself), **Chaos, Order, Souls, Nightmares**, and a female entity tied to "prophecy and spinner of schemes" ("way cool"). The destruction of the world was caused by a conflict — or vote — among the Watchers over "new stories": **"he got caged and another one got destroyed and that's why time's all fucked up."** He blames **Chaos** — *"Chaos hates Modeus"* (spelling varies across the session: Modeus/Mordeus/Modius/Moevious). A name, **"Nymdow,"** is also mentioned as having done something specific. The "shattered" god the party met recently is now described as "Shattered Glass."

**Halcyra's personal story:** a vision shows **"your father shaking hands with Refasual and signing a contract"** — explicitly NOT the party member Reficul; the name similarity is a coincidence/different entity. Oravis: *"this one was done without my knowledge... I thought the story your father was supposed to tell was going to be so much better. Boy was I wrong, it led to this story."* The major reveal: **"your father gave up his life to bring you back basically. That sickness, you died. Straight up, you made that, that's why, and the miracle child died."** This confirms: **Halcyra died as a child of an illness, and her father's contract cost him his own life to bring her back — she is "the miracle child."** Oravis: *"not going to kill you... it's growing on me... we're not going off script anymore."* Halcyra (quietly): *"You're the one that — no one else killed me... I'm sorry."* / *"She was a child."* A passing line — *"Chaos says, 'Oh, but I want her'"* (about Halcyra) — is flagged as a likely spoiler for later.

**Halcyra's mother:** a vision shows her alive and "perfectly okay" but unhappy, having fled aboard an airship called **"The Eagle"** (no eagle anywhere on it — the name is ironic), searching for her **"runaway daughter Hal Kyra,"** heading "all the way up."

**To Inquizo:** Oravis notes he's "trying to sneak off" (confirmed). *"You don't know it yet, but your arc... it ends beautifully. Tragically, but beautifully."* Halcyra starts to ask an unfinished question about whether there's "any way to... free... to tell you..." Oravis: *"you five have been handpicked for a good story... It wasn't just my choice."*

**Major prophecy reveal:** *"I helped the woman write this prophecy"* — Oravis refers to her as **"the arachnid"** or **"the weaver"** [NEEDS CONFIRMATION whether this is the Prophetess or a new entity]. Halcyra's prophecy scroll **spontaneously unfurls**, revealing a gold-highlighted line: **"Flesh runs living now decays."** Oravis: *"That's you!"* — confirming this stanza has been about Halcyra. He becomes somber, then delivers a cryptic directive: *"I've seen the person whose domain that is to tell you — Destroy the eldest God."* The party reacts strongly. Halcyra: *"I'm not one of you to destroy the eldest God... There are lambs for slaughter out there that do not become any single one of us."* This connects to "the eldest God," who was destroyed in the Shattering.

**Mira's major reveal:** *"Also, I believe it was fair to Mira to understand where her power comes from. The ancient god of chaos."* Mira's player protests: *"I am a wild magic sorcerer."* Oravis: *"Have you ever actually done wild magic?"* Mira works through "stages of grief," landing on rage: *"I am solidly in [rage]."* Oravis: *"It's okay. It's a stage of grief. She'll come too."* Mira: *"Wow, really diminishing your emotions, huh?"*

A side note: the chapter title for Numgis's eventual defeat is, per Oravis, simply **"The Balls Removed."**

**The Books:** "The Book of George" (Grungrak) "has already been written for a long time" and **has ended**. "The Book of Grundrack" — described separately — **has also ended**. "The Book of George" **will** end (an ongoing, future arc). And: **"There's a secret third book that I'm not allowed to tell you about"** (a player guesses: "Grundrack part two"). Inquizo negotiates and is granted **two secrets** he gets to keep. *"Inquizo's sweating! Trust me, guys, he gets better."* / *"That's definitely his mask"* — confirming Inquizo's goofy persona is a deliberate cover.

To Halcyra: *"Your mother, that's currently going on. That's a problem you need to figure out."* On the chaos-god connection: *"You can eventually, maybe... get all those names"* (implying a list of names tied to a future quest). **Halcyra receives "The Book of Criminals"** from Oravis — *"in exchange, this is going to make for a very interesting story."* He muses on fate: *"what if this was already written in the books and I just haven't read that chapter yet?... Maybe it doesn't even exist yet."* The door out won't open yet — *"my magic gobos haven't fixed the door yet."*

Oravis shows Mira a **sigil** that matches one already in her notes — *"his business card,"* made by him: *"I'm not an artist, I'm a reader."* Mira: *"You have no idea. I don't like the subtle wallbreaker. I am simultaneously enraged that this is how you reveal miracles."*

**Final warning** (delivered as completely genuine, not a joke): **"Be cautious around Alias."** Oravis references "Alias and Thorin's story" — *"when they saw the child and had to turn around."* *"The God of the Sea is super [plagued] with nightmares in case you forgot you had a drink"* — referencing a prior, unconfirmed encounter. *"Her good pal Thorin, what a guy."* **"You do realize that Thorin right now is kind of torn between two beings, correct?"** Thorin's true form is a "dad bod" (not the "ripped" fan-art version), and *"he loves one of his daughters."* The major reveal begins: *"What you see is kind of merged with one of your gods... yours! ... Thorin is the god of travel[ers]"* — directed at a specific party member (likely Mira) — and the scene cuts off mid-"Jeopardy"-style reveal of exactly which character's patron Thorin is merged with, continuing into the next scene.

### Scene 6 — The Crystal Realm and the Spider's Web (3:29:38–end, ~4:05:29)

The guessing game continues "Family Feud"-style — **"Music"** is confirmed as the correct answer for the Thorin/Hermes-merged deity. This merged god is **"super duper stuck right now,"** turned to statue [party has freed a similar statue before — "the one on the ship," NEEDS CONFIRMATION].

**Halcyra's resurrection backstory is expanded further:** her father's *original* intended deal was to "lead a charge that healed that specific sickness and eradicated it from your world" — a cure for **everyone**, not just Halcyra — but **"Moevious"/"Modeus"** changed the terms: *"went over my head... 'cause it was more chaotic"* — *"healing of disease... that's more in Modeus' realm — the balance in all things"* [this framing of Modeus sits oddly against his earlier "God of Chaos" association — flagged for later clarification]. Different "hells" per world are referenced [NEEDS CONFIRMATION whose]. A book "titled like ancient Greek myths" is given as "your story for **Domani**" [possible new deity, NEEDS CONFIRMATION].

**Major lore reveal:** *"You see, your guys' world, there was lightning that struck everywhere that was caused by that dwarf right there"* — pointing at George — *"as he struck that blue dragon with his hammer and it shattered... That was the ending of World One, ending of World Two."* George is, at this exact moment, **obliviously reading a music book** through the entire reveal. A third world-ending event is described: a forbidden ritual that "tore" reality between worlds ("paper tearing... I want to see paper written on"). **"Sibyl's Beast"** is confirmed to be real, "in the prophecy," and to have "stopped breathing" [significance unclear]. *"And so that was the end of three worlds"* — *"some of us gods... ripped apart the world and we put them back together"* — this is the literal origin of "**A Shattered World**": three torn world-maps, reassembled into one. The true author of the prophecy is "someone else" — not Oravis — likely "the weaver/arachnid." "Lots" of other worlds exist.

The party is shown a **crystal realm** with looping vision-panels: "Grungrac smacking the dragon" (on a loop), a giant Minotaur charging at someone, and a small child with a tall elf and a small halfling ("you have to protect children too?" / "Yep" — possibly tied to Liz's 15 children, or to another world entirely).

**Item acquired:** Halcyra uses her cartography tools (which she's had "since day one") to copy the composite three-world map — a **70% accuracy roll** ("pretty solid"). **Halcyra now has a hand-drawn, 70%-accurate map of the three original worlds.**

The transition out: *"the door is broken... You crawled into the spider's web."* The name **"Oravus"** appears here for the first time: *"Oravus grabs Refesiel by the back of his neck and just pushes him towards the spider web"* — **"Refesiel" is almost certainly a transcription error or name collision, NOT Reficul** [do not assume Reficul appeared this session]. "Oravus" is very likely the same entity as "Oravis" (a spelling variant). The name **"Jayreth"** is mentioned once by Halcyra's player [NEEDS CONFIRMATION]. The narrator teases, regarding "who my dad is": *"Not allowed to tell you who he is quite yet, he would be very proud of you,"* referencing a book titled **"The Ruffus Who Was Dad"** — the DM explicitly says, *"I changed the name so I don't spoil things."* No actual name is revealed (likely concerns Halcyra's father).

**The Spider Goddess encounter:** the party passes through "a really ornate door... spider motifs" into an illusion of their own airship — Dex is in the kitchen, and a figure called **"Mifara"/"Milifora"** (possibly the Prophetess [NEEDS CONFIRMATION]) is present. The illusion degrades — blurry, "tape scratched" — and the door closes behind them.

The true entity is revealed: **THE SPIDER GODDESS** — roughly 8 feet tall, "an infernal mixture of a spider and a woman... based off of a trapdoor spider," with far too many eyes. *"I so love[d] to finally meet you."*

**To Inquizo:** *"My dear, why do you still hide in this dream?... It's best that you sleep now. Lord knows you'll need it."* **Inquizo is magically put to sleep — and remains unconscious on the floor for the rest of the session.**

**To George:** *"Don't you wish to know who you are?... what if this mythical creature, this Grungrak as everyone calls him, is better than you? But also, what if in some way he is infinitely worse? ...who ends the world for one person? This is not a trait of yours."* George's only response: **"No."**

**To Mira:** *"...your world is unraveling?... you're so important to it. One could even say the catalyst."* A philosophical aside on prophecy follows — *"a script someone has given you... For to some the world has already ended... for others, it's the beginning of an effort."* Then, **the gift** — a new verse of prophecy, given directly to Mira:

> *"Because a pair of light is a single thing to find.*
> *It lies upon a path that lines.*
> *When monsters sleep, the dawn shall soar.*
> *The fated child to sleep no more."*

Mira asks whether this is part of the existing prophecy or something separate. The Spider Goddess deflects entirely: *"Maybe you should ask someone who will tell you... It also makes you more likely to do what I tell you to."* **The relationship between this verse and Halcyra's existing scroll is left explicitly, deliberately unresolved.**

**To Halcyra:** *"What is death but not a dream?"* Halcyra: *"Why does it have to be a dream?"* — and then Halcyra is **silenced**, her mouth physically unable to open (not the Silence spell, per the DM). The Spider Goddess continues: *"Do you not wish to know the terms of your own contract? Or do you not remember it?... you can hear the song, can you not? Even now it whispers through that life that you call your own. For some debts are collected by waking rather than signing. After all, what has more souls than that which keeps [strains/streams]?"* (final word garbled).

Her farewell: *"Regardless, this has been lovely... I hope you keep an eye out."* ("For what?" "You'll know.") Asked whether she's allied with "Librarian guy" (Oravis): *"That depends on which story is being told. But for this story you are with him. For this story, he's a good friend. I can do things he cannot. I can speak words that he cannot. I also don't like him, and he knows it... He definitely moves [stories]. He changes them despite the fact that he says he does not."* She's known him for "thousands of hundreds and billions of years." Halcyra's voice returns; asked if she has anything to say, **she says nothing.**

**Session ends:** the floor falls away, and the party finds themselves back on their actual airship — in the kitchen, which is completely **empty** (Dex and the Prophetess were both part of the illusion). The party is left shaken, and **Inquizo is still asleep on the floor.**

## Character Moments

**Halcyra** — Receives four stained-glass visions from the Machine (a blue dragon and hammer, a laurel wreath, herself among laurels, and the burning city of Erlon with her mother inside) — and chooses not to touch the Machine to learn more. Lands the killing blow on the Machine itself with a 17-to-hit Divine Smite. Fails, by a coin flip, to save Iskander from the shadowy figure, and watches Liz pull a gun and vanish from the party for good. Learns from Oravis that she died as a child, that her father gave his life in a contract to bring her back, and that her mother is searching for her aboard an airship called The Eagle. Watches her own prophecy scroll unfurl to a line that's apparently been about her all along — "Flesh runs living now decays." Receives the Book of Criminals. Is silenced by the Spider Goddess and told she has a "contract" with debts she doesn't remember — and, given the chance to say anything at all afterward, says nothing. Uses her cartography tools to draw a 70%-accurate map of the three shattered worlds.

**Mira** — Touches the Machine and triggers the session's largest vision: holding a giant scythe given to her by a statue of a goddess ("the Lady"), watching that goddess turn to stone, watching Argus fight off winged mechanical raiders, and hearing a mysterious "third voice" she's heard once before. Shares a private, lightning-charged vision with George involving a giant version of herself and a warning about an oncoming Cataclysm — both take 12 lightning damage exiting it. Is told outright by Oravis that her power comes not from a dragon patron but from the **Ancient God of Chaos**, and works through visible stages of grief on the spot. Receives a brand-new, four-line piece of prophecy directly from the Spider Goddess, who is called "the catalyst" by name and refuses to say whether the new verse connects to the existing prophecy at all.

**Inquizo** — Hog-ties the melting-skinned boy from last session's cliffhanger. Establishes a new houserule by rolling a natural 1 on Invisibility. Rolls Stealth 24 to scout the clock tower and overhears Dervnumgus and Gore Harlow's bargain (41% eavesdrop). Lands a double-attack that slices through every tendril in the room plus a bonus 8 lightning damage from his homebrew item — then immediately gets hit with "brain fog" and an urge to climb 200 feet to destroy the second eye. Recognizes Oravis/Aghaad with a Religion 16. Negotiates two kept secrets from a god. Is told his story arc "ends beautifully, tragically, but beautifully" — and that he has a brother whose name a god knows and won't say. Is put into a magical sleep by the Spider Goddess and spends the rest of the session unconscious on the floor.

**George/Grungrak** — Declines the Machine's offer to show him who he was. Lands the second blow on the Machine with Thunder Wave. Shares Mira's vision of "Grungrake" — a past self who didn't survive his own choice — and takes 12 lightning damage for his trouble ("Oh, it's lightning! No!"). Gains a level of exhaustion escaping the clock tower. Cannonballs through a goop monster with an Acrobatics 19, ending up completely covered in slime ("This is gross. I need a shower"). Obliviously reads a music book while a god describes, in detail, how George's past self ended two entire worlds with one hammer strike. Is told by the Spider Goddess that ending the world "for someone he cared about" is not a trait of his current self — and answers with a single word: "No."

## Lore & Worldbuilding Discoveries

1. **The Watchers** — an ancient pantheon of gods, each governing a domain: Stories (Oravis/Oravus), Chaos, Order, Souls, Nightmares, and a female entity tied to prophecy and "spinning schemes." A conflict among the Watchers over "new stories" — one got caged, another destroyed — is the root cause of "time's all fucked up."
2. **The Shattered World's literal origin** — three separate worlds were destroyed by three cataclysmic events (the dragon-shattering, a forbidden "paper tear" ritual, and at least one more), and a faction of Watchers tore apart and reassembled the three world-maps into the single world the party now occupies.
3. **Halcyra died as a child** — of a sickness — and her father's contract with an entity called "Refasual" gave up his own life to bring her back. She is, literally, "the miracle child."
4. **Halcyra's mother is alive and searching for her** — aboard an airship ironically named "The Eagle," heading "all the way up," looking for her "runaway daughter Hal Kyra."
5. **"Flesh runs living now decays"** — the gold-highlighted line from Halcyra's own prophecy scroll, confirmed by Oravis to be about Halcyra herself.
6. **Mira's true power source is the Ancient God of Chaos** — not (or not only) a dragon patron as previously believed. Mira is now grappling with this ("I am a wild magic sorcerer" / "Have you ever actually done wild magic?").
7. **The dragon-shattering reveal** — George's past self ("Grungrak"/"Grungrake") struck a blue dragon with a hammer, shattering it and unleashing world-spanning lightning that ended both "World One" and "World Two."
8. **A new prophecy verse was given directly to Mira** by the Spider Goddess — its relationship to Halcyra's existing scroll is explicitly left unresolved by the Spider Goddess herself.
9. **Inquizo has a brother** — Oravis knows his name and offers (ominously) to share it; Inquizo declines.
10. **"Be cautious around Alias"** — a genuine warning from Oravis about the established sea-god NPC (last seen beached and wounded, Session 13).
11. **Thorin (the statue god) is merged with another deity** — confirmed answer "Music," tied to one specific party member's patron [exact identity cut off mid-reveal].
12. **"Sibyl's Beast"** is confirmed real and present "in the prophecy" — described as having "stopped breathing."
13. **Halcyra's father's original deal was meant to cure the sickness for the entire world**, not just Halcyra — but "Modeus"/"Moevious" altered the terms to be "more chaotic," resulting in Halcyra's personal resurrection instead.
14. **New houserule** — rolling a natural 1 on an Invisibility-type check means you believe you are invisible when you are not.
15. **Homebrew item ruling** — Inquizo's lightning-damage item can be used a number of times per long rest equal to his proficiency bonus.
16. **The mind-control bug** is the same species/mechanism as the one from "Canaries' Gentle Descent" (Session 15).
17. **"The Book of George" and "The Book of Grundrack" have both already ended** — but "The Book of George" will also end in the future (an ongoing arc), and there is a secret third book Oravis is "not allowed" to discuss.
18. **The chapter title for Numgis's eventual defeat** is, according to Oravis, simply "The Balls Removed."
