# Letter — Aurum Eirien to Alistair Larkin

*A colleague writing to Larkin with concern about his deteriorating conduct and a demand for proper documentation.*

---

Alistair,

I have rewritten the opening of this letter three times now, which is already more ceremony than either of us ever tolerated in our academy days. You'll remember that we used to mock the senior lecturers for their long-winded reprimands filled of all posture and no substance.

I will spare you that indignity.

**You are making this difficult.**

Your conduct in recent council assemblies has not gone unnoticed, nor unremarked. You speak less, when once you led our discussions precisely. You were an ass, but you ensured we wasted no time. Now, when you do speak, it is displaced — as though the conclusion arrives before the argument has had the courtesy to introduce itself. That is not the man I studied beside, nor the one I have defended more than once in rooms where your name was spoken with skepticism.

And now, you withdraw entirely.

I am told your studies remain sealed, for at least days. That correspondence is delayed, truncated, or delivered through intermediaries who cannot answer even the simplest clarifying questions. I would ask you plainly: are these truly your words reaching the Council of late?

Because if they are, then I must be equally plain in return.

**They are insufficient.**

---

"DNA in metal," Alistair?

You, of all people, submit a proposal under a title so broad it borders on carelessness. There is no framework attached. No measurable hypothesis. No articulation of process, limitation, or risk. Only implication which I cannot fund, no matter how compelling it may feel in your own mind.

It cannot be that you are allowing an apprentice to draft your communications. And if it is not that, then I am left with the less comfortable possibility that you no longer feel the need to explain yourself at all.

That would be a mistake.

You know as well as I do that the Treasury does not fund arrogance.

This change… concerns me. I worry you may have discovered something you should not have.

---

Do you remember the east observatory?

Of course you do. You practically lived there. I recall dragging you out at dawn more than once because you had forgotten the rather basic human requirement of sleep. You would stand there, half-delirious, insisting that the patterns you were seeing would change everything — and that if you could just articulate them properly, the entire framework of mechanical theory would shift.

And the irritating part was you were often right.

But you never presented those ideas as inevitabilities. You had proof. You built them piece by piece. You let others follow your thinking. You made them understand. You were a better teacher than our professors.

That is what made you the essence of a luminary in my mind.

Now you send me fragments and expect me to see the whole.

**I cannot.**

And I will not pretend to. You used to make me feel so smart.

---

There is another matter, though I hesitate to commit it to paper.

**You do not look well.**

I am not speaking of fatigue. We have both worn that particular badge often enough to recognize it as something comforting. This is something else. There is a distance to you now, as though you are listening to something just beyond the rest of us.

If this is the cost of whatever work you have undertaken within that "Head Locker" of yours, then I must ask quietly and without accusation whether the cost is being paid entirely by you.

Or whether it may eventually be paid by the rest of us as well.

---

You know my position.

I will not authorize further funding for your classified research until I receive the following:

1. A clearly defined hypothesis, stated in terms that can be evaluated and replicated
2. A structured methodology outlining how "organic-metal integration" is to be achieved
3. Documented observations of current subjects (redacted where necessary, but not omitted)
4. A risk assessment detailing potential biological, mechanical, and societal consequences

You are capable of providing all of this.

Your failure to do so thus far is unsettling.

---

I will end this as I began — without ceremony.

You are not the man I remember, Alistair.

That may not be a failing. We are not meant to remain unchanged. But there was a time when your work felt like the beginning of something bright. Something that pushed against the limits of what we thought possible without losing sight of why those limits existed.

Lately, it feels as though that brightness has begun to dim.

A bright dream, perhaps, meeting the quiet insistence of reality.

Or something else entirely.

Send me the work.

Not just the fragments or the conclusions.

**The work.**

If there is something you cannot put to paper, or something that has found its way into your thinking and refuses to leave — then I suggest, as someone who has known you longer than most in that Council chamber ever will:

Talk to me face to face. I'll try to open your mind, one more time.

*Aurum Eirien*

---

## Notes

- **Aurum Eirien** — a Council colleague and old friend of Larkin's. Deeply concerned about the change in him
- **"DNA in metal"** — Larkin's research proposal that Eirien refused to fund; connected to Starglass and organic-metal integration
- **"Head Locker"** — Eirien knows about the secured research wing; she's asking pointed questions about it
- Eirien says "I worry you may have discovered something you should not have" — she suspects what's really happening
- **"Listening to something just beyond the rest of us"** — she noticed the contamination without understanding it
- This letter was written during Larkin's deterioration — before his Final Entry/suicide note
- Eirien is likely still alive; unknown if she knows Larkin is gone
