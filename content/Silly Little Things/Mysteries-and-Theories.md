## What Is Mira, Exactly?

**What we know:** Mira is dead — or something close to it. She is described as "reinvigorated by an unknown force" and "not supposed to be here." In the pit area she moved at 1/15th normal speed, her blood was tugged toward something, and she heard a voice say *You're not supposed to be here.* She does not know she is dead. It is not denial — it is genuine unawareness. Her character sheet lists her species as **"Human"** in quotation marks. She is a previous-campaign character who was pulled back into this world without explanation.

**Leading theories:**
1. Her father's bargain — *My soul for hers* — did more than save her childhood illness. It tethered her to the patron dragon's power in a way that keeps her walking even when she shouldn't be. She is not precisely dead; she is sustained by the contract. The contract is the only thing between her and whatever comes next.
2. Someone or something in this campaign needed her here specifically. The patron chose her before she was born, through her father's deal. She was placed back into the story by a divine hand that wanted her at this table, in this party, at this moment. She didn't come back on her own.
3. The Lethe sequence (Session 7) may have changed her further. She jumped in while charmed and saw duplicates of herself in the water. The River Lethe forgets everything — but what does it do to someone who is technically already dead? She may be more "here" now than she was before, or less. The effects on her specific condition have never been resolved.

**Why it matters:** When Mira finds out she's dead, everything she's done and chosen and built will suddenly be operating under a new question — *does it count?* And whatever is keeping her here will have to answer for itself.

## What Is the Devil's Deal — And What Is the Job?

**What we know:** Reficul made a deal with the devil. He cannot die — and cannot find peace — until a job for the devil is complete. After Session 7, he said he is 1/100th done. The deal explains why he goes to hell when he dies instead of dying normally; it is a permanent mechanic, not a one-time thing. He has died multiple times and come back. He would rather not talk about the dying. He has been 1/100th done for a while now.

**Leading theories:**
1. The job is something enormous — a century's worth of mortal work compressed into a single life. "1/100th done" is not nothing. It suggests progress is measurable, which means the job has defined endpoints. Maybe he is collecting souls, or changing specific people, or undoing something that was set in motion before the campaign started.
2. The devil's deal is connected to the campaign's larger prophecy. Reficul's prophecy stanza says: *A form untethered collects those who touch / Death does not hinder, for they escape its clutch.* He collects people who have been touched by something. The party might be the collection.
3. Staying in the island city either advances the job or puts it completely on hold — and Reficul does not know which. If the job is about people, about protection, about second chances — then what he's doing in the island city could be the job. But if the job is something specific the devil named, then every day he spends there is a day lost.

**Why it matters:** When the job is done, Reficul gets peace. And peace might mean death.

## Who — Or What — Is Mira's Patron?

**What we know:** Something has been speaking through Mira since at least Session 4, when a masculine voice emerged from her instead of her own when she cast Toll the Dead. Her pact weapon is a sickle that manifested on its own during the Pmurt fight. She is a warlock who did not choose to be a warlock. In Session 12's shadow therapy, the patron revealed itself: a blue dragon with green veins. Inside the dragon, Grungrak was present. The contract: *My soul for hers,* signed by her father when Mira was five and dying. The dragon has held that contract ever since.

**Leading theories:**
1. The dragon is a specific named entity in this world — a deity or near-deity who trades in soul contracts and manifests power through those who are owed to it. It has been with Mira since childhood. She did not notice because she never knew to look.
2. Grungrak's presence inside the dragon is not metaphorical or incidental. They are from the same world (SKRP), and something from their shared origin connects them at a level neither has fully understood. The dragon may not be purely Mira's patron — it may be something that holds *both* of them.
3. The patron is not the dragon. The dragon is just the form it chose to show Mira because that was what her shadow could hold. Whatever it actually is predates the campaign, predates Mira's illness, and predates the deal her father made.

**Why it matters:** Mira knows the origin of the contract now. She has never confronted the patron directly. At some point, she will.

## What Doesn't Halcyra Know About Herself?

**What we know:** Halcyra is the daughter of Apollo. She does not know this. She is the child of the overarching campaign prophecy — the one meant to fuse the three fractured worlds. She does not know this either. She has been searching for the "chosen hero" described in a prophecy she's carried since age 15. She has not connected herself to the hero in the scroll. In Session 12, Apollo called her name three times in a dream, described as "familiar, kind," and said *the worlds are at stake.* She walked toward the voice. She does not know whose voice it was.

**Leading theories:**
1. She already knows, somewhere below conscious thought. Her wings drooped during the formal judgment at 18 — her body betrayed the shame of who she was before she understood why. She has been searching for a chosen hero who matches a description that fits herself exactly. The gap between knowing and not knowing may be thinner than it looks.
2. Someone deliberately kept this from her. Her mother had something related to her father — something Halcyra left without receiving in the Session 12 dream. That item is still in her mother's hands. The withholding was not accidental.
3. When she finds out, it will not feel like revelation. It will feel like something snapping into place that was never quite right. The Bow of Dusk will become the Bow of Dawn. The party will have to catch up with what she suddenly understands about herself and all of them.

**Why it matters:** The prophecy says the worlds can only be fused by the hero — and the cost will be something irreplaceable. Halcyra is going to be asked to pay that cost without being told what it is first.

## What Will the Pomegranate Seeds Do to Grungrak?

**What we know:** In Persephone's forest, in the underworld, Grungrak ate multiple pomegranate seeds. In Greek mythology — which this campaign runs on — eating pomegranate in the underworld binds you there. Persephone herself ate six seeds and must spend six months of each year in the underworld as a result. Grungrak has eaten multiple seeds and is now out of the underworld. The DM knows what this means. No one at the table has said it aloud. Nothing has happened yet.

**Leading theories:**
1. The seeds are a slow fuse. The underworld has a claim on Grungrak now, and at some future moment — perhaps at the worst possible time — that claim will assert itself. He will be pulled back. He will be called. And unlike Persephone's six months, nobody knows the terms of Grungrak's deal because Grungrak didn't know he was making one.
2. The seeds are the prophecy's hammer. His prophecy stanza says *When hammer breaks on the dragon's scale.* He's been carrying the shards of his hammer. Maybe the pomegranate seeds are the mechanism — the thing that breaks the hammer, or breaks him, or forces a confrontation with whatever fate was set for him underground.
3. The seeds are something Persephone allowed because she needed him to carry them back out. She's a goddess. She knew what he was eating. If she didn't stop him, she either didn't care, or she wanted this outcome.

**Why it matters:** There is no version of this where the pomegranate seeds are nothing. The DM flagged it. It is waiting. The question is not whether it matters — it is what happens when it does.

## Who Is the Doctor?

**What we know:** The Doctor is an unknown figure who arrived at Malafera's home, caused violence, took her daughter Starglass, modified her and installed her as the airship's living breathing apparatus, and traded the ship to King Pmurt in exchange for his crown. He was in communication with Numgis. His dossier papers on the airship are written in two handwritings: one colonial and journalistic, one in Malafera's rune alphabet — and the second voice becomes violently hostile toward the cave civilization near Sypium. He was described by Starglass as *"a different man underneath his skin."* His hat and mask (labeled "dr.l") were left behind on the ship. They are now in Inquizo's pocket.

**Session 14 update — his name:** His travel log was found aboard the ship. His full name is **Dr. Alistair Morgenstern Larkin**. "dr.l" was his initial all along — L for Larkin. Lieutenant Galveston called him **"the head luminary"** — a title, not just a name. He was expected at Bermuda Foundation. He was not there. Every building in the city was on fire when the party arrived.

**Leading theories:**
1. He is literally a different person underneath his skin — a shapeshifter, a possessed vessel, or two entities sharing one body. The two handwritings in the dossier are not a stylistic choice; they are two different minds. One of them is Malafera's, or something that learned her language by force.
2. He is connected to the same unnamed deity referenced in the ruins pages — the one Malafera may be connected to. Whatever is behind the goop constructs and the rune alphabet has a face, and it is the Doctor's.
3. "The head luminary" is a rank, not just an honorific. It implies a hierarchy — which means there are other luminaries. Galveston transformed into an insectoid horror the moment the party appeared. Whatever the Doctor's people become, it is not something they chose. He may have done to Bermuda Foundation what he did to Starglass.

**Why it matters:** He is ahead of the party. He has already reached Sypium. He wants the cave civilization destroyed. And whatever he is underneath his skin, the party has his hat — and now his name.

## What Is the Rune Alphabet — And What Happened to Malafera?

**What we know:** The circles-and-clouds writing that has appeared since Session 8 — on house walls in X-salent, on wagon bottoms, in abandoned town murals, and now on Starglass's body — is the language of Malafera, Starglass's mother. The runes mean "hello" and "goodbye" to her constructs. The last word she taught was "life." She created the goop substance, the constructs, and the cave creature the party killed in Session 10. She has "a lot of children." The Doctor arrived at her home, caused violence, and she is now missing — ran, was taken, or is in hiding.

**Leading theories:**
1. Malafera is alive and has been watching the party since Session 8. The writing on the walls is not a record of something past — it is her still communicating. She left the signs for someone who could read them, and the party has been following her breadcrumbs without knowing it.
2. She is a captive. The Doctor, or whoever sent him, needed her ability to create constructs and he took it. She's been held somewhere since before the campaign started, and the constructs that have been appearing are the last echoes of what she made before she was taken.
3. She is not an enemy. She is a creator who lost control of what she built — or whose creations were weaponized by someone else. The cave civilization near Sypium may be her people, or made of her people, and the Doctor's violent hostility toward them is about destroying her.

**Why it matters:** Every piece of the mystery substance, every goop construct, every rune the party has encountered traces back to Malafera. She is at the center of the thing they've been walking toward for the entire campaign.

## What Is Inside the Second Glass Case?

**RESOLVED (Session 14):** The case was opened by Halcyra (nat 20). Inside: a burned humanoid creature with exposed muscle, white irises, and many eyes — an unsettling presence that emerged from a larger multi-eyed floor organism in a room that shifted from foggy blue glow to warm sunshine. It communicates by bending wires to spell out words. It was holding the sun navigation medallion. When asked to give it up, it rolled its eyes and spelled *"obviously."* Very sassy. Very reluctant. Did not seem pleased to have visitors.

**What's still unknown:** Where this creature came from, what it is, and why the Doctor had it in a glass case under an Apollo tapestry. The sun pendant it was holding was a navigation medallion — but whether that's its whole purpose or just what the Doctor used it for is unclear. It lives on the ship now. Nobody has asked it its name.

**The Apollo tapestry question remains open.** Sun, rays, wings, sheep — the case was hidden under specifically that imagery. The creature may have no connection to Apollo whatsoever, or it may be the entire point. The tapestry was a cover, not a coincidence. Someone put it there deliberately.

## What Happened to Mira's Father?

**What we know:** When Mira was five years old and dying of an illness, her father signed a contract: *My soul for hers.* A blue dragon with green veins — Mira's patron — holds that contract. The dragon has been with Mira ever since, manifesting as a masculine voice through her death magic, then as the pact sickle, then in full form during Session 12's shadow therapy. The shadow called the necklace Mira received her father's "final gift" — which implies he is gone, or unable to give anything else. Mira put the Stormwake Sigil on immediately after waking.

**Leading theories:**
1. He is in hell. The contract traded his soul — and a soul that has been traded for service ends up below. He is down there somewhere, in a place the party has already visited, in a form they might not recognize. The word "final" may mean he is gone rather than simply absent.
2. The dragon has him. The patron who holds the contract may have taken his soul as a living captive rather than simply consuming it. He exists somewhere inside or adjacent to the dragon — the same space Grungrak briefly occupied when the dragon manifested. Mira did not look for him when she walked into the dragon. She may not know to look.
3. He is alive somewhere in this world, and the contract's terms are that he lives — diminished, soul-sold, but present — for as long as Mira does. The necklace is not a posthumous gift. It is something he released to her at the moment she finally found out the truth.

**Why it matters:** Mira knows the origin of the deal. She does not know what happened to her father. She has been quiet about it since Session 12. She will not stay quiet forever.

## What Are the Pomegranate Seeds and What Is the Serpent Watching Inquizo?

**What we know:** A serpent has appeared specifically watching Inquizo across multiple visions: in Lorya's nail image (staring directly at him), in Arcus's nail, in the glass at the mines (George saw it and lied about it), and on Richard's spoon. The jade/emerald necklace Inquizo gave Urscylla also depicted a snake eating itself. Grungrak and Inquizo have both encountered the serpent's gaze without fully acknowledging it.

**Leading theories:**
1. The serpent is Urscylla, or an extension of her. It appeared most clearly in the nail images, which came from the blood angel — a denizen of Hell. Urscylla sits at the Gate of Ivory, pinned under a boulder, probably not dead. The necklace Inquizo gave her may have been a recognition: she saw the serpent in him before he saw it in himself.
2. The serpent is something much older — a watcher from outside the campaign's cosmology, tracking Inquizo specifically. His prophecy stanza speaks of illusions and spinning wheels and secrets. Something has been paying attention to him since before this story started. It is not threatening him. It is waiting for him to figure out what he's supposed to do.
3. The serpent is connected to the Chains of Eternity and the Warden Brother. His shadow showed him his sister and his brother — both unresolved. The serpent may be a symbol of that entanglement: the thing that bites its own tail, that holds itself down, that is both trap and freedom. The chains on his wrists are the same design.

**Why it matters:** Something has been watching Inquizo longer than the campaign has been running. It has not introduced itself yet.
