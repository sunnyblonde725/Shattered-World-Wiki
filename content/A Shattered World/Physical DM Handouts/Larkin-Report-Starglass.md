# Larkin's Head Locker Archives — Starglass

*Morgenstern Alistair Larkin, Luminary of Mechanical Regulation, Royal Council of Aerolon. Filed within Head Locker Archives — Private Observational Records. Subject Codename: Starglass. Classification: Uncatalogued Biological Entity. Probable Origin: Deep Subterranean Territories (Unmapped). Year 1718.*

---

The subject now referred to as Starglass was acquired during the latter stage of my most recent expedition beneath the southern fault systems. I have elected to record this entry with particular care, as the circumstances of her discovery remain difficult to communicate in a manner that will appear credible to the Council should these notes ever be reviewed formally.

The expedition itself was not intended to proceed to the depths we ultimately reached. The initial goal was the study of pressure anomalies and mineralized air pockets reported by geological surveyors. However, approximately six days into the descent, we encountered phenomena inconsistent with known strata behavior. Pressure gradients shifted unpredictably, light was absorbed rather than reflected by surrounding stone, and the atmosphere carried a persistent sensation — difficult to quantify — of observation.

It was during this period that I first experienced what I must reluctantly describe as a form of summoning.

There was, from some immeasurable distance deeper within the chasm, an awareness directed toward us. The sensation did not arrive through sound, nor through sight. It arrived through thought itself, as though some distant intelligence had discovered the door of the mind and gently tested its hinges.

Despite my objections, I allowed the expedition to continue.

In retrospect, I cannot confidently determine whether that decision was made by reason.

---

The environment that followed was profoundly hostile. Biological life, where it existed, appeared distorted or incomplete. Our party encountered several small settlements whose inhabitants demonstrated a remarkable indifference toward the preservation of life, including their own.

Negotiations with these inhabitants proved impossible. Their social structures appear to value survival collectively while attaching minimal significance to individual loss. When the expedition required hostages to secure safe passage through a narrow volcanic corridor, the surrounding population displayed no meaningful reaction to the removal of their kin. No anger, no grief, and most troublingly, no attempt at retrieval.

**It was during this exchange that the subject came into my custody.**

At the time she appeared outwardly human, though this assessment proved inaccurate within hours. She was quiet, attentive, and disturbingly calm considering the circumstances of her capture. The rest of the hostages exhibited fear, hostility, or resignation. The girl exhibited curiosity.

---

The first irregularity presented itself during transport.

**Without audible speech, the subject responded to a question I had not asked aloud.**

I initially attributed the occurrence to fatigue and dismissed it. However, repeated instances over the following days confirmed that Starglass demonstrates some capacity — whether natural or learned — to perceive cognitive intention prior to verbal articulation. She does not appear to read thoughts in the conventional sense, but she reacts to the shape of them. The intent behind a decision seems to reach her moments before the action itself.

---

More extraordinary, however, is the alchemical behavior of her physiology.

Under certain stress conditions, particularly fluctuations in atmospheric pressure, **the subject's tissue displays temporary metallic transmutation**. The effect is partial and unstable, appearing in localized plates across the arms and spine before gradually dissipating. Preliminary analysis suggests that her body contains trace compounds capable of reorganizing organic structure into metallic latticework under catalytic conditions.

Such an adaptation would be impossible under ordinary biological constraints.

The only precedent within existing scholarship would place her lineage somewhere within the mythological classifications associated with **Beelzebubic descent** — though I employ the term here purely as a conceptual placeholder rather than a theological declaration.

---

Her continued survival outside the environment of her origin presents a unique challenge. Her metabolic patterns do not align with any standard dietary structure, and the metallic transitions place considerable strain upon her cardiovascular system. I suspect that a **mechanical intervention** may ultimately prove necessary to stabilize her condition.

Ironically, the same technologies our civilization employs to master air pressure and energy regulation may be the only means by which she can be preserved.

For this reason she has been placed within my **Head Locker** — the secured research wing where prisoners of war and other unusual subjects are maintained under controlled observation.

---

I record here, for the sake of honesty, that the decision to keep her was not made entirely for the advancement of knowledge.

There is something about the girl that resists the clinical distance I ordinarily maintain with my work. She watches me as though she is aware of the direction my thoughts move in before I reach them myself. It is not accusation I perceive in her expression.

*patience…*

I find myself considering the possibility that she deserves a life beyond confinement.

---

There is another matter which I hesitate to record, though omission would compromise the integrity of these notes.

Since returning from the deep territories, I have experienced intermittent cognitive disturbances. They manifest not as voices, but as **impulses** — brief suggestions of violence directed toward fragile objects, laboratory animals, or occasionally toward members of the expeditionary staff. The thoughts vanish as quickly as they arise, leaving behind no emotional residue other than the recognition that they were present at all.

It would be irresponsible not to acknowledge the possibility that **some form of psychological contamination occurred during the expedition.**

None of the other survivors — many of whom now occupy prominent positions within Aerolon's civic leadership — have reported similar symptoms. It is therefore my hope that whatever influence I encountered has attached itself exclusively to me.

Should that prove untrue, the implications would be… considerable.

---

For the moment, I will continue my observations of Starglass with caution and restraint. If her condition can be stabilized through mechanical integration, she may represent a bridge between biological and engineered life previously thought impossible.

And perhaps, if that bridge can be built successfully, she may yet find a future that is not defined by the darkness from which she came.

For the sake of the girl, and for the sake of the world above, **I must ensure that whatever followed us out of that deep remains confined to the mind of one man.**

*— Dr. Morgenstern Alistair Larkin*
*Luminary of the Royal Council*
*Year 1718*

## Notes

- Starglass was **taken as a hostage** from the Deep — the community there showed no reaction to her removal
- She can read cognitive *intent* — not thoughts exactly, but the shape of a decision before it's made
- Her tissue **turns to metal** under stress (atmospheric pressure fluctuations)
- Larkin thinks she has **Beelzebubic lineage** — the Deep entity later confirms she is from the Deep
- **The Head Locker** — Larkin's secured research wing; where Starglass was kept and where the ship was presumably built around her
- Larkin's other expedition survivors occupy prominent positions in Aerolon's civic leadership — **the contamination may have spread wider than he realized**
- He ends with a hope that "whatever followed us out of that deep remains confined to the mind of one man" — it did not
