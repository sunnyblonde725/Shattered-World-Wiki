## The Prophecy — Annotated

*The full prophecy text lives in [[Prophecy]]. Below is a stanza-by-stanza breakdown of what each section likely means based on what we know so far. Some are confirmed. Some are speculation. All of it matters.*

---

> *A child born at the end of the world,*
> *Their fragile form in chaos unfurled.*
> *In fractured fields where shadows creep,*
> *They wake the gods intended to sleep.*

**→ Halcyra.** The child of prophecy, born as the worlds were colliding. Her very existence is waking things that were meant to stay dormant — including, possibly, Apollo himself.

---

> *The sun withdraws, its gaze turns cold,*
> *No warmth remains, no hand to hold.*
> *The child forsaken left alone,*
> *To find the light and find a home.*

**→ Halcyra / Apollo.** Apollo (the sun) has withdrawn — his gaze has turned cold. Halcyra's divine parent is absent. She has been left to find her own way without being told who she is or what she's for.

---

> *When hammer breaks on the dragon's scale,*
> *When the circle falters, rites shall fail.*
> *When the written fate in shards is cast*
> *Cybele's beast shall breathe its last.*

**→ Grungrak + the shards.** The hammer breaking likely references Grungrak's missing war hammer. "Written fate in shards" connects to the shards Grungrak carries. Cybele is a goddess of mountains, wild beasts, and nature — her beast dying may signal a major world event triggered when these conditions align.

---

> *When lightning flashed and paper tore,*
> *A god's machine shall roar no more.*
> *The fractured fields in silence weep,*
> *Dreams of fire consume the deep.*

**→ Unknown — future event.** A divine machine going silent. "Paper tore" may echo written contracts (the mermen's fishbone contracts?) or a literal tearing of fate. Still to come.

---

> *A world from ruin made,* / *The broken world in a shattered frame...*
> *The thrones of heaven crack and fade...*

**→ World state stanzas.** These describe the collision of the three worlds (SKRP, Planetos, Anthropocene) and its consequences. "Fragments of Gods that could not die" directly references the stolen statues — "Poppa Poseidon" and the "God of the Seas" — divine artifacts now in human hands.

---

> *Look upon those who stand,*
> *With the hero, hand in hand.*
> *Their hearts shall guide, their courage lend,*
> *For through their strength, the fates shall bend.*

**→ The party.** The prophecy acknowledges the people standing with Halcyra. They are not sidekicks — the fates bend through *their* strength, not just hers.

---

> *A shattered mind turns towards the gate,*
> *One must reverse the dwarven fate.*
> *Paths once set shall twist and bend,*
> *Only courage may amend.*

**→ Grungrak.** "Shattered mind" = his amnesia. The "dwarven fate" is whatever destiny was set for him before he forgot who he was. He must actively reverse it — face something, reclaim something. His courage is the mechanism.

---

> *Flesh once living now decays,*
> *Yet walks the earth in cursed arrays.*
> *Bound by shadow, life unknown,*
> *Lives their life, secretly alone.*

**→ Mira.** She is dead. She walks. She doesn't know. "Secretly alone" — the secret is kept even from herself.

---

> *A mind broken by design,*
> *Holds the rites of the divine.*
> *Memories tangled, hidden from time,*
> *Whispers echo fate's true crime.*

**→ Grungrak (possibly) or a character yet to be fully revealed.** "Broken by design" implies the amnesia wasn't accidental — someone or something caused it deliberately. He holds divine knowledge without knowing it. This is significant.

---

> *A form untethered collects those who touch,*
> *Death does not hinder, for they escape its clutch.*
> *Souls adrift in shadow streams,*
> *Bound by neither hopes nor dreams.*

**→ Reficul.** A changeling — literally untethered in form. Death does not stop him; he escapes its clutch by going to hell and coming back. "Souls adrift in shadow streams" may describe what he encounters in hell, or what he carries.

---

> *Upon the wind, hidden wings shall rise,*
> *A shadowed flight beneath uncharted skies.*
> *Though tethered still by mortal chains*
> *The hero soars where fate remains.*

**→ Halcyra.** Her wings are hidden. She has been hiding them in trench coats, being passed off as a pet, keeping them folded. But the prophecy says they will rise. She is tethered by mortal chains — she doesn't know her divine lineage yet. When those chains break, she soars.

---

> *One fated to fuse the fractured fields,*
> *Wandering too close to see their yield.*
> *Their light shall burn, yet shadows stay,*
> *The cost of wholeness cast away.*

**→ Halcyra's burden.** She is the one meant to fuse the three shattered worlds back together. But she is "wandering too close" — she can't see the full picture from inside it. And wholeness has a cost. Whatever she must give up, it is real and it is permanent.

---

> *The ring where colors spin and dance,*
> *Illusions weave and take a chance.*
> *Beneath the tent where laughter hide,*
> *Spinning wheels and secrets bide.*

**→ Inquizo / future location.** The illusions, spinning, laughter under a tent — this reads like Inquizo's energy, or a location the party will visit. A circus, a carnival, a place of deception dressed as joy.

---

> *Heal the faces made of stone,*
> *Defeat hateful hearts upon the throne.*
> *Through fire's trial, truth is shown,*
> *The hero reaps what they have sewn.*

**→ ALREADY IN MOTION.** "Faces made of stone" = the defaced god-statues (face carved off, replaced with the tribe leader's). "Hateful hearts upon the throne" = Queen Ariel (defeated, Session 3) and likely the island tribe leader (still to come). Restoring the statues is an active quest thread.

---

> *The healer's hand to bind the seam,*
> *Through golden light to cure the dream.*
> *What once was broken now shines bright,*
> *As shadows yield to blinding light.*

**→ Halcyra's paladin purpose.** Her healing hands, her golden divine light — this is her role in the endgame. She is the one who literally binds the seam between the worlds.

---

> *Beneath the mountains, giants sleep,*
> *In spite of efforts, they lost their sheep.*
> *One step shakes both stone and sky,*
> *Fates that tremble where they lie.*

**→ The mysterious white-haired NPC / Session 3.** Giants sleeping beneath mountains. The white-haired NPC grabbed a green sheep during the Session 3 encounter — "they lost their sheep." One step from a giant shakes stone and sky. This entity is tied to something ancient beneath the earth.

---

> *Where Hades' veil and sunlight meet,*
> *The child shall walk in realms replete.*
> *By Gods' design both lost and found,*
> *The child treads on sacred ground.*

**→ Halcyra between worlds.** She will walk where death and the divine intersect — possibly literally. "Both lost and found by Gods' design" echoes Mira's condition too. Sacred ground means the pit, or somewhere like it.

---

> *Upon the throne where shadows reign,*
> *The crown is forged in loss and pain.*
> *To claim its power, none stand free,*
> *For chains of gold still bind the sea.*

**→ Ariel (partly fulfilled) + future.** The underwater throne was Ariel's — forged in grief over Eric. But "chains of gold still bind the sea" — the mermen's contracts may not be fully resolved by her death. Something still holds.

---

> *The lost wander where shadows creep,*
> *Their silence echoes, secrets keep.*
> *Yet through the dark, a plan devised,*
> *To find the one who in dark lies.*

**→ Reficul in hell.** He is lost in shadow. His silence echoes. But he is not idle — he is devising something. He will find, or be found by, someone in the dark.

---

> *Stand where woven threads divide,*
> *One course to drift, one course to guide.*
> *Both bear sorrow, both bear pain,*
> *Neither leaves the world the same.*

**→ The final choice.** Halcyra will face a fork. Neither path is painless. This is the moment the whole campaign builds toward.

---

> *Only through loss will the fractures close,*
> *Only through silence the true song grows.*
> *The hero must give what none can spare,*
> *Through surrender, the worlds made fair.*

**→ The ending.** The worlds are healed through sacrifice — Halcyra must give up something irreplaceable. The nature of that sacrifice is unknown. But surrender, not victory, is what closes the fractures.

## The Story So Far

### The World That Broke

Three worlds exist — SKRP, Planetos, and the Anthropocene — and they have collided. Not slowly, not metaphorically. They have shattered together into one shared space, and the seams are showing everywhere. The sea has secrets buried under it. A field exists where a dead woman's feet stick to the ground. A man arrives on a gust of wind with rose bones and hyenas at his command. Everything about this world feels like it was supposed to be somewhere else and got caught in the break.

Somewhere in this broken world, a prophecy is unfolding. It centers on a girl with wings who has no idea. Her name is Halcyra. She is the daughter of Apollo and the child of the prophecy, and she is currently hiding her wings in a trench coat and hoping nobody notices.

The five people traveling together — Halcyra, Grungrak, Reficul, Inquizo, and Mira — did not come together by plan. They came together by pressure. A pirate said get in the submarine or die, and here they are.

---

### Session 1 — A Tavern, a Carnival, and a Kingdom That Doesn't Know It's Wrong

Five strangers met in a tavern. Not dramatically. Not by destiny's obvious hand. Just: a drunk dwarf was singing at the bar, an invisible kobold tried to steal a rock, and the rock floated — because invisible kobold, not invisible rock — and that was enough.

The dwarf's name, when asked, was George. It wasn't. His name is Grungrak. But Mira walked over and said "Grungrak, is that you?" and he looked at her and said "No. My name is George." On the spot. The name stuck. The history it was deflecting did not go away.

Mira and Inquizo had been passing through. George, Reficul, and Halcyra were on a quest to get George's memories back — Reficul had found him in a cave (under circumstances that still haven't been explained) and Halcyra had inserted herself because she was eighteen years old and believed she could fix people. She went upstairs and sold every pillow in the inn. This is not metaphorical. This is Inquizo.

The next morning they went to a carnival. Inquizo stole a catastrophic amount of cotton candy and spent the day as a floating pile of it. Reficul found the strongman game surrounded by a growing semicircle of dwarves, watched a child ring the bell with a rigged hammer, and directed the resulting crowd-of-dwarves-carrying-a-chosen-one into an impromptu trapeze circus with a Nat 20 performance roll. Human cannonball dwarves. Then red fog rolled into the big top. Hooded figures appeared. Everyone went to sleep.

They woke up shipwrecked on a beach.

The beach had stairs. The stairs led up to a gatehouse. The gatehouse guarded a kingdom with a very confident worldview: only humans exist, and only this kingdom exists, and their king — King Pmurt, a man too large and too stubborn to walk — was in charge of all of it. The kingdom didn't speak to women. Reficul did the talking. He asked to meet the king. They let him through.

King Pmurt complained about fish and birds and people being annoying, then floated away on his chair. His vizier, Durf Numgis, walked them down a hallway and began explaining that everything — truly everything — is about sex. This was a lot for Halcyra at eighteen. The conversation escalated into a full argument and Numgis cast a Nightmare Spell. George went catatonic, muttering about lightning and pain — his trauma too real for a nightmare to improve on. Reficul's vision placed him before the Duke of Hells being dressed down for his choices. The price of ending it was calling Numgis *mommy*.

From the balcony, Numgis offered his floating ship. The party jumped off the railing instead.

They landed on the second beach — the one around the corner they had walked past — and found the pirate women. Beautiful. Armed. In possession of very large cannons. They wanted something from inside that kingdom. The party had just been inside that kingdom. The logic was irresistible.

In the dead of night: Mira turned herself in as a prisoner. Inquizo went to break her out. George and Halcyra grabbed one of the four statues from the town square — the one with a fishtail — and used it as a battering ram to burst the city gates open. Reficul tried to melt the castle's gold plating with Heat Metal. It didn't do much. But a butcher appeared beside him in the chaos, said *"there are some of us who agree with you,"* and vanished back into the crowd.

One statue. One new debt. One more job.

*"Now we have another job for you."*

### Session 2 — The Submarine, and the Queen

The party was already in debt to the pirates before they got in the submarine. They knew the island — they had already seen the stolen statues, seen the tribe of men who resented anything not human, seen the statue with the tribe leader's face carved over the god's. They knew more going into the sea than the sea knew about them.

The pirate captain's second ultimatum — get in the submarine or die — was the push that sent everything into motion.

What followed was chaos dressed up as a diplomatic mission. Reficul became a shark. Grungrak nearly drowned in two feet of water. Inquizo stacked three hats, one of which says DO NOT TOUCH. Mira read the entire user manual while the floor was opening under her feet and labeled every button on the submarine's control panel with parchment and quill. This is who these people are.

The underwater city was a society built on control. The queen — Ariel — was both ruler and god, which meant her word was law and her cruelty was divine. She had been silencing "inferior" creatures for years. She had been maintaining a city of people who couldn't speak freely. And when the party showed up with genuinely useful intelligence — the island tribe has your stolen statues, the pirates are running retrieval operations — she declared combat anyway.

That is the kind of queen she was. That is the kind of grief she carried.

### Session 3 — The Queen Falls, and the World Gets Stranger

The throne room fight was brutal, fast, and full of reveals. Ariel charmed half the party immediately. Reficul stabbed himself and said "see you in hell." Grungrak was asleep in a bar making a fish cry with songs.

But when Ariel died, something cracked open.

A silence that had covered the city for years — a suppression spell, applied broadly and without mercy to every creature she considered beneath her — dissolved instantly at her death. Hamish the puffer fish historian found his voice. Every silenced creature in the city could suddenly speak. One act of violence ended years of enforced silence. That is significant. That is not just a combat victory.

The party left the city with a body (Reficul's true form: small, horned, long white hair, blood that shifts green to blue with temperature), a named historian (Hamish), and more questions than they arrived with. Reficul went to hell. He will come back. He always does.

Then they walked into a field and everything got weirder.

The pit is something. The glowing white strings, the mud-slow movement, the voice that said *"you're not supposed to be here"* — this is not a normal location. This is a wound in the world. And the person it targeted specifically was Mira, who cannot heal others or pray in that space, and moves through it like she's walking through the wrong side of a door.

Mira is dead. She doesn't know it.

That sentence — delivered casually mid-chaos — is the emotional center of the campaign so far. Whatever reinvigorated her, whatever force decided she should be walking around in a broken world that has a literal pit that rejects her — that force has not introduced itself. And Mira is just doing diplomacy and labeling buttons and being competent, completely unaware that the world is slowly trying to push her out of it.

Then the white-haired man arrived. Shirtless. Pine cone. Rose wings. Hundreds of hyenas deferring to his word. A nail in his kneecap that bleeds green foam. The party gave him laxative bubblegum and managed to avoid combat entirely by being too chaotic to fight. He left northward. He will be back too.

The Good Boy of the North laughed from the northern forest. The party prayed to it and it heard them. The moon is paying attention.

---

### Session 4 — Into Hell, Through the Temple, and the Blood Angel Rises

The party followed the white-haired man's path into the abandoned farmland and found something worse than a monster — they found a family history.

The buildings were rotting, empty, covered in fungus. A nursery with no children. Murals of a winged figure descending, nails raising the ground. Inside a locked chest: a black orb containing an imprisoned entity of unknown identity, who speaks in contradictions and described the prison as "a terrible box without a blankie." (The entity's name was spoken as "Caelus" during the session, but Ian later confirmed it is not Caelus — identity still unknown.) Inquizo made contact and got his consciousness pulled inside. His body kept walking. Grungrak carried it.

Beneath the field was a marble temple with 247 sheep and a statue of a fertility goddess — Cybele, also called Avani — wings cut off at the ankles, wrists, and back. And a sickle in a stone cairn. The sickle is sentient. It called itself Vengeance. Against the sky. Against love lost. When Grungrak grabbed it, his eyes went red and he sprinted directly at the enemy before the pre-combat speech could happen. Bloodlust, persistent, pulling.

The sickle also revealed the real names of their two companions: Arcus (formerly "Just Me") and Lorya (formerly "Reasonable"). Children of Cybele. The sickle said: *"The last time someone like you went down there, they didn't come back up. Who wielded you then? My son. Arcus did."* He came back eventually.

They descended through Lorya's cloak and fell into ankle-deep blood. The sickle confirmed: down is Hell. A silhouette of half an angel rose from the blood — one arm, one side of a face, a half-halo, nails in its right palm only. It had orders: make Arcus and Lorya suffer for the next couple hundred years. Children of a cheater, paying for their mother's choices. The blood angel was their half-sibling, through the father — Caelus — who divorced Cybele and took one child with him while the other two stayed with their mother.

Halcyra took both the angel's arms off with a natural 20. Mira cast Toll the Dead and a masculine voice came out of her. The angel said *"You're already mine. I have a number. It's 8. And I'm not alone."* Then it claimed Mira's memory of life before her illness — her pre-sickness childhood, gone — and the session ended mid-combat on a Phase Two cliffhanger.

### Session 5 — Lorya Dies, and Mira Tears the Door in Half

The blood cocoon reformed. Caelus spoke through it — *"Are you one of her two?"* — and when Grungrak said no, cold wind grabbed him by the neck and threw him out. Grungrak cast Calm Emotions and the angel went docile long enough for Halcyra to interrogate it. This is where the family lore came out: the divorce, the orders, the centuries of intended suffering. Then Mira's patron took over her body. Dark ink tendrils from her fingernails flung the angel back into the spikes and the calm broke.

Lorya was nailed into a blood cocoon. Grungrak reached in — Strength 21 — and pulled out her nail. She appeared for a moment. Horrified look. Then she was gone. Lorya is dead. Inquizo pulled the last nail from the angel's chest and it dissolved into goo.

The blood realm retreated, replaced by a cave of nails. The party walked deeper and Inquizo walked face-first into a mirror wall showing his reflection holding a dagger. He found Lorya's serpent-circle jewel on the ground and took it. Grungrak kicked him through. Everyone followed.

They emerged into a floating archipelago suspended in purple-black space — the Lost Place, a pocket dimension with clipboard workers, lightning slides, glass mines, and a cult leader named Richard running everything. He had been trapped there 472 days. His entire spoon table was cocaine, mushrooms, opium, and acid. Inquizo sneezed on all of it.

Grungrak cut off an overseer's hand at the mines. A Duergar miner kissed his hand and cried. Grungrak also saw the serpent in the glass and lied about it. Halcyra rolled Insight 22 and caught him immediately.

The wheat seeds they needed were gone — thrown out by Baleam, who couldn't grow them in infertile soil. The plan shifted: wait for the wheat field exit to cycle back. That night, Grungrak had a vision — a face in a puddle of water, a name: *"Reficul."* He said he was going back in the water to find them.

Richard confronted them at dawn with a crowd and two chained women. Halcyra scattered the crowd with Cause Fear. Richard made his save, pulled out a wooden revolver, and asked if they wanted to work for him or die. Inquizo stabbed him and hit something solid under the graduation robe. Something supernatural lives under there. Halcyra hit the crowd with Thunder Wave. The party sprinted through the portal.

Mira grabbed the sickle. *"Vengeance, do you want to tear a hole through reality?"* The sickle said yes. She slashed the portal in half horizontally. The crowd ran through in matching top and bottom halves. Stewart the green sheep fit under the cut entirely unharmed because he is simply too short.

The party landed in the Fields of Asphodel — a wheat field the size of a sky, under a stone ceiling with mechanical spotlights sweeping the rows. A farmer named Reficul stood in the field, completely unfazed by the pile of bisected cult members, and introduced himself. He knows the way out. He knows Stewart. He has the wheat seeds. And the field belongs to Ariel — the queen the party killed in Session 3 — by permission from Hades.

### Session 6 — The Horse Has an Axe, and Grungrak Ate the Pomegranate

Session 5 ended with a farmer standing in a wheat field in hell, surrounded by bisected corpses, saying *"Welcome to the Fields of Asphodel."* Session 6 began with Mira tackling that farmer, because the farmer was Reficul, and Reficul has been in hell since Session 3.

He had been on "vacation." He was a shark. He got mistaken by another shark for the one it was chasing. He ended up in the underworld. He found a boat. He found a map. He had been exploring. This is completely consistent with who Reficul is, and somehow makes everything better and worse at the same time.

The reunion was warm in the way that reunions are warm when one party has been dead for several sessions. Mira tackled him. Grungrak didn't know who he was. Inquizo denied all knowledge. The party then proceeded to get on Reficul's boat and nearly die in the River Lethe.

The boat had a problem. The "anchor" was a metal horse. The horse had an axe. Nobody knew this until Grungrak went below deck, found what he assumed was the steering wheel, applied Strength 24, ripped it out of the floor along with the floor, and launched himself backward in an arc toward the forget water — toward a river where, if you land, you remember nothing. Halcyra caught him. The horse woke up. The horse had an axe.

What followed was a combat aboard a listing boat in the underworld between the party and a metal horse wielding a great axe in its teeth. Earth Tremor brought the horse and Reficul prone. Burning Hands did five fire damage. Halcyra's radiant bolt rolled a one, bounced off the horse, and hit Reficul in the face for seven. Then the horse sat down on the hole it had partially caused, closed its eyes, and went to sleep. The horse solved the boat problem by being heavy. This is the level of chaos that this campaign has normalized.

Without a steering wheel, the boat drifted into the Shrine of Persephone. Reficul failed to get off in time. The boat landed on him. He died, as he does. His changeling child form appeared, then a seven-foot naked man erupted from the black sand and announced himself. Grungrak, furious at having watched this twice now, picked up the sickle and walked into the white forest.

The forest was pomegranates and white trees with black leaves. Every tree Grungrak cut transformed into a dryad at the last second and ran screaming toward the building at the forest's edge. The forest cleared. Pomegranates covered the ground. Grungrak, still angry, sat down and ate one like an apple. Then more. Nobody stopped him. The table knew what this meant. The character did not.

The percentage die said the sickle had 73% control over him. The dryads inside were holding protest signs that said "KILL HIM."

Then Persephone walked out.

She is twelve feet tall. She has a three-headed puppy (Cerberus, proportional to her, which makes it enormous to anyone else). She has Truesight, which means the Invisibility Inquizo had cast an hour ago was immediately visible to her. She ensnared Grungrak with vines and dragged him toward the building without ceremony. She does not negotiate with people who are standing. She negotiates with people who are vine-bound.

The party talked fast. Mira explained — they are not dead, they ended up here by accident, the sickle was controlling Grungrak, they want to leave. Grungrak tried calling her "really pretty vine lady." Persephone, against her better judgment, found this charming. She told him to keep calling her beautiful. He said: *"I will call you beautiful."* He meant it.

She would help them. Two conditions. First: the sickle. Persephone knew what it was. She wanted it. The sickle that Grungrak has carried since Session 4, the one that whispers in his ear and causes bloodlust and craves flesh, was handed to Persephone. It is gone. Whatever the sickle was going to do with Grungrak, that story has ended. What it does with a goddess of the underworld is a different story.

Second condition: her niece. Ariel — the queen the party killed in Session 3 — has been swimming through Persephone's soul pool looking for her husband, who drowned trying to breathe underwater to be with her. The husband's name is Eric. He was in the pool. Persephone needed him out.

Reficul dived into the soul pool to retrieve him. He died. He came back. He dived again. He died again. On a different set of dice, he found a semi-corporeal form, and Eric surfaced.

Persephone snapped his throat open so he could speak. Eric asked about Ariel. Ariel is dead. The party told him this in stages, some intentional, some not. Eric found out his wife is dead. He also found out who killed her. He is processing this. He is a ghost. He does not have great options. The plan is to go find her — she's "somewhere else" in the afterlife, not here. Eric can run a sailboat all night long. This is the skill the party needed most when they were on a sinking boat twenty minutes ago, but timing is what it is.

Persephone snapped her fingers. The party was at the bottom of an elevator.

The underworld has layers. Below: the Fields of Asphodel, the rivers, the shrine beaches. Above: a city. Skyscrapers. Trees wrapped around buildings. Roads of asphalt and roads of flowing water with boats on them. An upper civic level that feels like Venice built inside a mountain. The Gate of Ivory is here. The Psychopomp (Thanatos) docks here — tall, antlered, perpetually drowsy, guides the dead to the appropriate exit.

In the elevator, Grungrak composed "The Ballad of George" — an adaptation of "The Ballad of Grungrak," retitled for Eric, performed live to Eric in the elevator, in the underworld, while a metal horse pressed all the buttons with the flat face of its axe. Eric contributed the second verse. It is, by all accounts, a good tune.

They boarded a new boat. The Psychopomp took the helm. The water road led toward the Gate of Ivory. The gate was close.

Then Urscylla surfaced. Six dog-heads above the waterline. Six tentacles below. Three on each side of the boat. Initiative was called. The session ended there.

The party is close to the exit. Grungrak ate pomegranate. The sickle is gone. Eric is with them. The horse is with them. The gate is right there.

### Session 7 — The Boulder Rolls, and Chapter 2 Begins

The battle with Urscylla was not clean. It was never going to be clean.

Halcyra cleaved a tentacle off with thirty-seven radiant damage on a natural twenty. The tentacle fell into the water like a tree coming down. The voice in her head said *"please let me taste that divine voice of yours"* — intimate and hungry and wrong. Halcyra did not waver. This is who she is.

Then Urscylla started singing.

Inquizo and Mira both failed their saves. Inquizo, charmed into trust, cycled through his collection of interesting objects — the penis rock, the wrong rock — and eventually landed on the necklace. The jade/emerald design of a snake eating itself, or a bird. He held it out. Urscylla smiled. Eels don't smile, but this one did. It took the necklace gently in its teeth and sank. Whatever that necklace was, it is gone now, and whatever it meant to Inquizo — or to whatever has been watching Inquizo — went with it.

Mira heard the voice say "get in the water." She got in the water. The river around them was the Lethe and the Cocytus mixing — Grungrak's persuasion failed, his Healing Word failed catastrophically, and his voice disappeared into the air instead of reaching Mira at all. The logic was impeccable: Healing Word is verbal. He said a spell. His voice left.

In the water, Mira saw versions of herself. The Lethe does that — it holds your reflection up until you can't tell which one is you. And then Ariel appeared. She distributed pearls — River Styx pearls, small and purposeful, one for each of them — and Eric appeared. The ghost of a man who drowned trying to breathe underwater for the woman he loved, crying out at the sight of her. *"It can't really be you, can it?"* Ariel pulled out a stone. The stone said, in some form, "come here, babe." Eric read it and jumped into the Lethe without hesitation. Grungrak lunged. Ghosts don't feel like anything. Eric was gone, the Lethe's surface smooth over the place where he'd been. Ariel turned to the party, told them their friend was waiting at the cliff, threw a coin, and swam away.

By the time she disappeared, she'd already forgotten who Eric was.

That is the shape of the Lethe. It takes the thing you came here with and gives you the water back.

Then Grungrak tackled Charon into the river too, which was possibly a tactical decision and possibly a reaction to watching Eric disappear and not knowing what else to do. Either way: the ferryman went in. Grungrak followed, headfirst, because a coin flip said so. The Lethe held him, and showed him George — himself, a reflection with a name, reaching out a hand.

Grungrak grabbed it. Natural twenty on the self-acceptance roll. Of course it was.

The Anglite rock that the duplicate was carrying sparked against the water, against Urscylla, against whatever the Lethe decided was happening — and an explosion fired them both up and out of the river like a cork from a bottle. The boat went with them, aimed at the cliffs, and Reficul appeared at exactly the wrong moment: close enough to be the first thing the boat found. He said "Crush the—" before the hull landed on him. D4 for luck: two. His beard got a trim. His body went on another brief vacation.

Grungrak landed like a superhero. Inquizo landed like he'd planned it. The horse was fine. The boat was done.

Five tentacles still above water. Urscylla's main body walked out of the sea.

Grungrak caught Reficul's voice — an arcana check and a dexterity check and a glowing orb that said, in Reficul's voice, *"George, George — big run. Heave."* It was not a poem. It was instructions. Grungrak looked up at the cliff and saw Sisyphus pushing his boulder. And he understood.

The boulder went over for the first time in history. Result of thirty. Sisyphus's eternal punishment, interrupted by a dwarf with his pinky toes still mortal and something to prove. The boulder landed on Urscylla. The glowing orbs flew free. The voice came home.

It is raining on the other side of the cliff. Just Sisyphus's tears.

The party crushed the pearls — each one dissolving the body into something between alive and ghost, leaving only the weakest part mortal. Grungrak's pinky toes. Inquizo's ear fins. Urscylla's last water jet hit Grungrak for twenty points of damage before the pinning, pulling him back into his body before he could fully ascend. They passed through the Gate of Ivory anyway.

They leveled up. And Charlie took the DM seat.

Chapter 2 started like Chapter 1: with a portal, and somewhere on the other side of it.

X-salent is a guild town — flat, wide, heraldry depicting two crossed feathers. Mira knew it immediately. Grungrak felt it with the back of his skull — flashes, the way something familiar arrives when you're not ready for it. Seven years ago, they were here. Then they fell through portals and didn't come back. Now they have.

The world has changed while they were gone. Mountains where there weren't mountains. Volcanoes. "Huge arcs of lightning broke the land" — six months ago, while the party was somewhere between the underground temple and the Fields of Asphodel, something happened to the surface world that left permanent marks. The worlds are still moving. The shatter didn't stop.

Justin at the guild office gave them room keys with the affect of a man who has decided that events simply are what they are. Mira's old room: disposed of. Reficul's records: damaged, bottom half gone, including his birthdate. *"It just says gone."* Justin did not elaborate. Justin never elaborates. The party signed in as "The Incredible Mistakes" because Horrible Mistakes was already taken, and because it is the most accurate thing anyone has ever called them.

The cartographers had a quest about a saltwater river that was stopping too early. A woman somewhere in the city was begging anyone to come deal with a monster. And Halcyra had something in her bag that she'd carried since she was fifteen.

In the tavern — where Penopolis the sentient pine cone served food and asked no unnecessary questions — the honest conversations finally happened.

George asked Reficul directly: *"How is it that you can just die and come back in two minutes?"* And Reficul, after a long pause and a half pint, told him. A deal with the devil. A job that must be completed. Peace withheld until the task is done. He cannot die until it is finished. He is one hundredth of the way there after everything that just happened. 

Grungrak revealed that he relived dying in the Lethe — the lightning, the dragon from the beginning, again and again until he wanted to forget.

George told him: *"You died. Dying is not your fault."*

It was probably the most useful thing anyone has said to Reficul in a long time.

George also told the rest of the group, in the only way he knows how — by being honest and slightly petty — that the vacation joke had gone on too long. That he cared deeply about them. That treating him as someone who would simply forget a friend had died was the specific wound. *"I care deeply about you. Acting as if I would forget — that's not all there is to it."* Nobody argued. Mira went quiet.

And then Halcyra pulled out a piece of paper.

She had grown up feeling judged by how she looked. At eighteen, her wings fell — something happened, something that left a mark — and she had been searching ever since. Following a prophecy she grew up hearing. Looking for the chosen hero it described. The paper was the prophecy. The party was about to read it together when the session paused, because it was time to go see the Hunchback of Notre Dame.

But the door is open. The paper is on the table. And the party that was assembled by pressure and piracy and coincidence is sitting in a tavern at the start of Chapter 2, seven sessions in, about to find out what they were assembled for.

### Session 8 — The Writing on the Wall

The party slept in pieces. Grungrak did not go to look for Mira. He found the stage, and Penopolis, and a pint (or two, or three past the guild limit), and fell asleep in the tavern like a very tired piece of luggage. Inquizo spent the entire night going door-to-door. Not looking for Mira — doing a petition. For the river. For their quest. Getting signatures from people at 3 a.m. who, against all expectation, signed. He covered 10% of the city. He did not find Mira.

Reficul and Halcyra searched.

They found her at 8 a.m., when the sun was barely up, having knocked on the door of every wrong building in the city first. Mira said "just go away." Halcyra asked to come in. Mira said it was unlocked.

What Halcyra said in that room — *"You're my friend, first of all. I'm worried about you"* — was not a speech. It was just the truth, stated plainly, by a person who has been carrying enormous weight in complete silence for the entire campaign. Halcyra is the child of prophecy. She is the daughter of a god who abandoned her. She has been following a piece of paper since she was fifteen years old, searching for a chosen hero she has not yet found, carrying a weight she has never put down. And what she chose to do with the first quiet moment available to her was sit down next to Mira and say: I came because you're my friend.

She told Mira about the prophecy. About George — how he's felt right from the beginning, how she can't explain why, how she keeps taking care of him because something in her says she's supposed to. Mira said she couldn't help right now. Halcyra said: *"That's understandable. Come get me by end of tonight."*

Reficul left a grandfather clock outside Mira's door. Silent Image — a projection, intangible, with a sticky note that said "You need time XOXO" and a smiling face. He waved it away when Mira smiled at it. This is the emotional register Inquizo operates in: gestures, chaos, and a quiet disappearing act when things get too real.

They walked into the poorer part of the city because there was nowhere else to be. The houses pressed together. The guild was behind them; the world got smaller the further they walked from it. Reficul bought a terrible hand-carved totem from a street vendor for three gold, convinced the vendor to sign a contract making Reficul his unpaid legal counsel, received the vendor's room number as a "business card," and walked away with a piece of wood that did not protect against anything.

Then Grungrak heard a scream.

He ran. Mira was closer and ran faster. What they found in the wealthy district near the Guild Hall was a woman sitting on the curb outside a Victorian manor, shaking, surrounded by city guards. She had woken up to find writing on the wall of her bedroom. Two-foot letters in a black, dripping substance that had been wet when applied. This was the twelfth time this had happened in twelve days.

The guard read the accumulated text aloud. The creature in it *scuttles, slithers, twists, and decays.* It is *lying in wait* past the gate, in the woods. It feeds on the shattered, the blessed, the defamed. Every instinct in the room — in both Mira and Grungrak, and later the whole party — recognized those words as a category that includes them.

Mira investigated the room. The wall writing had no arcane energy. The window frame did — fine sigils in the same substance, in an unknown script, the writing of someone who knew what they were doing and did not want to be found doing it. She took a sample. She noted the script. Grungrak took the guard's notebook and copied the poem into his songbook, because if there is going to be a song about a scuttling thing that hungers in the woods, Grungrak is going to be the one who writes it.

The guard told them about a merchant. Arrived months ago, covered in mud and scratches, claiming a monster had chased them from the woods. Fine glasses, now shattered. Darker complexion. They spread flyers — the same ones the party had been seeing all over the city. Groups searched the woods and found nothing. The merchant stopped being a person with a story and started being a problem: raving, refusing escort, insisting the creature was at the edge of the tree line. Two weeks ago, they disappeared. Rumor says they ran into the woods screaming.

Two weeks ago. The writing started around the same time.

Meanwhile, Reficul and Halcyra were walking back through the slum district when Halcyra spotted someone. Fifty feet away, moving through back alleys — quick, deliberate, running from the direction of the Guild Hall. Reficul reached out without moving. Telepathy: *"Divinity needs your haste. Meet the tavern in two hours."* It was the most cryptic, most completely Reficul thing he could have possibly said to a stranger fleeing through a slum.

He tracked them. Survival roll of 22, Detect Magic on the mud, following a trail that ended at a wall of homeless people where one of them was sitting with a gray blanket pulled over their head, breathing wrong. Reficul approached. *"I will heal you."* The person said they were quite fine. Reficul reached out with Starry Wisp to mark them — a tracker, invisible, harmless — and the person smacked his arm away.

What followed was a diplomatic failure in the most specific sense of the word. Words of Terror: the person barely held their nerve. They kicked Reficul into the mud. Reficul, from the mud, cast Vicious Mockery with Psychic Blades — twelve damage, the person clutching their head — and reached for his rapier as the community around them began to rise.

Grungrak used Calm Emotions. It worked on half the crowd. The mysterious figure used something else entirely: they vanished. No trace except the blankets they'd been sitting under, and the distinct absence of anyone who had been there a moment ago. Halcyra and Grungrak saw it happen. Mira and Reficul did not.

Reficul cast Earth Tremor.

Four people went to zero. Four people who had been sitting against a wall, in a city that did not take care of them, protecting a stranger they considered one of their own. They dropped. And Reficul's devil's deal curse chose that precise moment to fire: he transformed into an Oni. Large. Blue-haired. Red-eyed. Standing in the middle of a public square in X-salent, covered in mud, with a tiny rapier tucked somewhere on his person.

Halcyra cast Command: *"Approach."* He came.

Mira and Grungrak got there in time to save all four people — Spare the Dying, Healing Hands, the cantrips of a bard and an alchemist who have both had to patch up worse. Mira gave the remaining community members gold from her own pocket, apologized on behalf of the group, and watched a Black dragonborn grab her arm as she turned to leave. He let go. He sank back into the wall. She took note of his face.

The dressing-down she gave Reficul afterward was surgical. *"Have you considered that they were going to protect one of their community members from a stranger who was verbally berating him?"* Reficul said yes, and that was why he had cast Spare the Dying. He was, he noted, surprised by how brittle their bones were.

Then Mira and Grungrak walked away together. Grungrak suggested a long conversation about a mystery might help. But that wasn't what happened yet.

Mira reconciled with George. That's the correct way to say it — she was the one who made it real, who held the space for everything that needed to be said, who accepted and extended in equal measure. She had been there when Grungrak died. She remembered it — the blue dragon, the explosion, the only thing left being the hammer core and a sound that was already fading. She was told to run and he listened, and she has regretted that every single day since. She lost a friend to a stupid sacrifice very recently, and he wasn't ready to face the fact that she had let another person do the same thing she swore she would never let happen again. Pretending Reficul was stuck in the ocean was easier than knowing she had watched him stab himself and dissolve into sludge.

She also said: everyone he has ever loved is dead or might as well be. And she said it the way you say a fact you've been carrying for a long time, not the way you say something to wound someone with.

Mira received all of that. She acknowledged she had been too aggressive trying to help him remember. She said she understood about avoiding instead of denying — that pretending he was trapped was easier for her too, for a while. She was the one who made the acknowledgment real, who gave it back to him as something they shared instead of something she was dispensing. And Grungrak said, quietly, that he had met Grungrak in the Lethe river. That he was kind. That he could see why he was her friend.

*"I met him in a river. It was weird."*

He asked her to someday tell him the tale of a jolly good fellow. She said yes. He pulled her into a bear hug with the strength of a bear — she is taller than him by enough that it hit somewhere around his hips — and they stood there in the street while Inquizo was long gone, having slipped away (Stealth 16, nobody noticed) the moment anything became emotionally serious.

The session ended with the party in pieces again, but a different kind of pieces. Mira heading to the alchemy guild with an ink sample. Grungrak heading to the blacksmith for a hammer that felt like a hammer, not a rock. Halcyra resting in Grungrak's room, exhausted from forty-eight hours awake and whatever it costs to be the calm center in a room full of chaos. Reficul, still an Oni, still unresolved.

And somewhere in the city — in the two hours Reficul had promised — a door was either opened or left shut.

### Session 9 — The House That Screamed

The party split, as they often do, into its most natural configurations: the person who does research went to do research, and everyone else went to find trouble.

Mira spent the day working. Not the desperate, cobbled-together investigation of the night before — actual work, with guild alchemists and a goblin librarian and a full table of testing equipment. She found two people willing to help her at the Alchemy Guild: Altair, an elf, careful and methodical, and Leander, who was enthusiastic in a way that made the word *explosive* loom nearby. Together they confirmed what she had suspected but couldn't prove: the substance was not from SKRP. It behaved wrong. It beaded up when heated. More importantly, it reacted catastrophically with something called Agopyl — the inner spongy membrane of maple stems, produced by a condition called the Syrup Virus, which is itself caused by a mineral nobody has been able to identify or classify. When the substance contacted Agopyl, it turned gaseous. When salt was added to the gas, it solidified into something that looked like graphite. No alchemist at the guild had ever seen a reaction profile like it.

Mira documented everything, handed it over as an official record, and went to the library.

The city library had what she needed: a book called *Ruins from Ancient Ages*, pages 35 through 40. A goblin librarian named Ny'e — with notably tiny legs and a precise professional air — processed the checkout without ceremony. The pages confirmed, in the dry tone of an author who knew exactly how extraordinary their conclusions were, that the ruins marked on the party's map were not archaeological. Not architectural. Not natural. They were purely magical in origin, and they were linked to a deity. The author refused to speculate between two possible candidates. They did not name either one. They noted only that there were two options, and that naming them would be irresponsible.

Mira also copied 18 plant entries from a foraging guide, because Mira is a person who uses her time well.

Meanwhile, Reficul — still an Oni, still large and blue-haired and visibly not normal — ended up in the Victorian manor district with Grungrak and Inquizo at some point in the afternoon. The houses there were the ones covered in writing: the same script, the same black substance, exterior walls full of the poem the guard had been copying into notebooks for twelve days. Reficul walked into one of the houses.

Inside, they were not alone.

There was another adventuring party already there — a dragonborn with a glowing purple rapier, a tiny mouse who was apparently a full party member and not a pet, a minotaur in mage robes, and a sorcerer woman who would later mention, almost offhandedly, that her warlock companion had just left one day and hadn't come back. They were exploring. So was Reficul. A confrontation began.

Then the dragonborn's rapier shifted into a warhammer and his eyes went red.

Not metaphorically red. Not emotionally compromised red. Red, glowing, the way eyes glow when something is using them that isn't the original owner. He went berserk. The weapon changed shape in his hands. The mouse party member pinned Reficul with an invisible boulder. The house was doing something to the people inside it, and at least one person was not going to be able to walk out the same way he walked in.

Grungrak cast Sleep.

The dragonborn — below 30 hit points by that point, whatever the house had been feeding on — went down. The room went quiet in the way rooms only go quiet when something large that was very loud stops being loud. The party ran. And the house, as they cleared the door, *screamed.* Not metaphorically. Not the sound of a door on hinges. An actual scream, audible and wrong, following them out into the street.

They left the dragonborn asleep on the floor. He was still there when the door closed.

Before all of this, or possibly after — the timeline of the spaghetti incident is difficult to reconstruct — Reficul found rancid spaghetti hidden inside a hollow brick in the manor's wall. This is not something a person plans to find. He found it anyway. Inquizo and Grungrak, with the speed of people who have been waiting for an opportunity that has finally arrived, attempted to use it as a religious prop for an ascension ceremony on the sidewalk outside the haunted house. At some point the brick became an improvised weapon and struck one of the adventuring party NPCs in a way that cannot be explained by any theology the party practices.

After the escape — after the house had screamed, after the spaghetti had been deployed, after the dragonborn had been left unconscious on a cursed floor — Grungrak pulled Reficul aside.

*"I believe in you,"* he said. *"You told me — everyone deserves a second chance."*

He used Reficul's own words. Not to wound him — to mean it. Grungrak is many things: chaotic, forgetful, liable to find rancid pasta in a wall and treat it as a gift. But at the bottom of all of it, he is the person in this group most likely to say the true thing plainly, without irony, and mean it. Reficul has been telling people their lives have worth since the campaign began. Someone finally told him the same thing back, in his own language.

The emotional impact of that is his to process. But the moment was real.

Halcyra had spent the day elsewhere. In the temple district, at Apollo's temple, with the YOMI papers — the crossed-out letters from the mystery bundle she has been carrying, the ones that read *please help me* in a handwriting that looked familiar in a way she couldn't name. She prayed. And the letters began to glow.

Heat, first. Then light. Both together, rising from the paper like the paper was remembering something it had forgotten. Mira was there — she confirmed arcane energy, rolled 12, nodded in the way someone nods when a thing they expected to be strange is strange in a way they expected. Two theories emerged and neither resolved. Either Apollo responded — either the god she was praying to heard her and reached back, in the only language he could use, through letters she wrote to him that she doesn't remember writing. Or the ink in the YOMI papers is reactive, like the mystery substance, and the candles in the temple did something to it chemically.

Neither option is comfortable. Both options are interesting.

It is 8 PM in-game. Prophecy Day did not happen. The party is heading to Reficul's apartment for a long rest. In the morning — finally, after everything — they are going to the forest.

### Session 10 — The Thing That Eats

The party woke up at different hours and ate omelets. This sounds ordinary until you know that Reficul had been awake since 2 AM, failed his self-imposed push-up contract by two reps, and decided the correct response was to cook for everyone. It is a very specific kind of person who channels pre-dawn guilt into apologetic breakfast food. The omelets were apparently good.

Before setting out for the forest, they played a get-to-know-you card game — the spin-the-bottle kind, with questions. What came out of it was not small.

Reficul described the first time he picked up an ink and quill. His hands moved on their own, he said. He learned to write before he could speak. The writing was never legible to anyone else. He said this without drama, as if it were simply a thing that had happened, but everyone in the room knew what they were looking at: a child who was marked before he understood what marks meant. The same substance that now covers the walls of X-salent’s Victorian mansions and coats the bodies of dead animals in the woods apparently decided, long ago, to express itself through the hands of a changeling boy who couldn’t even talk yet.

Halcyra said she was 18. She said she left home at 18, over a disconnect with her mother about her father. Her father is Apollo. She didn’t say that part out loud — she doesn’t know it yet — but the shape of what she described was a girl who grew up knowing something was wrong with the way the sun worked, and a mother who couldn’t explain it, and a daughter who eventually stopped waiting for an explanation and left. She also said, quietly, that she believed George was part of the prophecy. That she had sworn to protect him. She did not say how long she had known this.

George talked about Grungrak. The real one. He said he was the most enthusiastic person he’d ever met. That he found George when he first arrived at the guild and decided they were going to be best friends before George had any say in the matter. That he was a talented storyteller. “And now it’s in our river,” he said, and stopped.

That’s the thing about George. He knows exactly who he’s a copy of. He carries it alongside the version of himself that survived, and most days he doesn’t put it down.

---

The caravan was blocking the gate when they left.

It was from Velodorn — a city no one had heard of until five months ago, which is when the world broke. The wagons were full of bedding and silk. The people were fine. The horses were fine. Every piece of cargo was coated, instantly and completely, in black inky goop the moment the wagons crossed the city threshold. Mira threw salt. It hardened like burnt caramel. Detect Magic read Transmutation school, tied to the gate boundary itself. The caravan driver was, reasonably, upset.

No one knew what to do about it, so the party went to the forest.

The trail was clearly made by someone who was frightened. Hardened candle wax pressed into the ground, each drip marking the frozen moment of someone moving too fast to hold a candle still. Broken branches at intervals, all at the same height — someone running, not walking, not looking back. Dropped torches, burned out where they fell.

The cave at the end of the trail was an old bear den, maybe 35 feet across. Inside: a dead man. Seven torches, all burned out. One still going. A journal, open on the ground.

They recognized him. He was the missing merchant. He had been missing for two weeks, and he had been here the whole time, dying in a cave surrounded by candles while something in the dark waited for him to stop moving.

They were still processing this when the thing came through the wall.

---

It moved the way ghosts move in stories — pressing through solid matter, insubstantial — but it wasn’t a ghost. It was a No-Face from a nightmare. It passed through the party at the entrance, walked directly to the body, and began eating it, and as it ate it became real. Visible. Present.

The DM described it as a fucked-up deer. Chronic wasting disease aesthetics, but worse: serpentine elements, a second head starting to form from the first, claws like a bear’s, fangs like hypodermic needles. Made entirely of the black inky substance. Not undead. Not a demon. Not a celestial. Halcyra’s Divine Sense came back blank on all three. It was something new, or something very old that the current classification systems hadn’t accounted for.

It hated light. When Mira hit it with Burning Hands, it screamed in multiple voices — some of them drowning, some of them wet in a way that suggested a mouth too full of teeth — and flailed desperately at a fire that wasn’t actually burning it, trying to put it out. Halcyra lit a torch with a cantrip and the thing recoiled. What it was afraid of was being seen.

When it bit Halcyra, something happened that was worse than the wound: she lost a spell slot. Not to damage. Not to an effect. The creature had reached in and taken a piece of her magic and eaten that too. This is what it was built for. Not to fight. To consume.

Then it grabbed Inquizo in both mouths and ran.

Inquizo, to his credit, fought back. He rolled a 1 and two 3s. He missed absolutely everything. He was in the mouth of a goop creature with two heads and he flailed heroically and achieved nothing, which is a very pure summary of who Inquizo is as a person.

What saved him was George. George ran up to Halcyra mid-combat, touched her on the shoulder, told her she was a hero, and then sang something so beautiful that it healed both her and Inquizo by nine HP each. Then the creature — still carrying Inquizo, still bleeding nightmare substance from where the party had been hitting it, still terrified of the light — made a mistake. It turned its back on Reficul.

Reficul, who was currently a kobold.

The devil’s curse had activated again: fourth time, random form, coin flip result, kobold. And the particular mechanical property of kobolds is that they can walk through other creatures. So Reficul walked through everyone, through the creature, positioned himself directly behind its skull, and launched a mote of radiant light at point-blank range.

Both heads exploded. The body crumpled like a wet bag and turned to goop where it fell, and Inquizo landed on the ground, and the cave was quiet.

---

The aftermath was not clean.

Halcyra and Inquizo both had bite wounds with black goop spreading out from the punctures. Cure Wounds pushed some of it out, but dark shadow marks remained at each entry point — the deep-ache feeling of a shot that hadn’t finished working itself out. Mira collected goop in a bottle and confirmed via salt test that it was chemically identical to the wall writing and the caravan substance. She collected skull shards. She collected the fangs. She grabbed the journal.

The body of the missing merchant was gone. The creature had eaten him completely.

On the walk back, the DM noted that the goop was slowly absorbing into the earth. By the time anyone came looking, there would be nothing there.

At the gate, the Alchemist Guild was still arguing with the caravan about agopyl quantities, and the Enchanter’s Guild was crouched under the wagons with special eyeglasses. George overheard the enchanters before anyone else: they had found runes on the bottom of the wagons. Same alphabet family as the ruins. The same script that Mira had spent a full session researching — the purely magical ruins linked to a deity no one would name. Someone had put that language on the bottom of a merchant caravan from a city that didn’t exist six months ago.

The DM translated the runes, loosely: *sleep. return. catalyst. invite.* And then, in the same sentence: *dinosaur cheesecake.* The language had never been formally deciphered. Things got lost in translation. But the coherent words were coherent enough. Something had been invited. Something had been asked to return.

George said it out loud at the end: *I think there is someone or something making goop constructs in the woods.* Nobody disagreed.

One player, more quietly, asked: *Is this the Nightmare King?*

No one answered that one.

### Session 11: The King's Breakfast

The party split before they ever reached the island city.

Mira and Reficul went through a portal at midnight — a narrow window, operated by a gender-fluid figure who let them through without ceremony. The portal delivered them to a city operating under a regime of fear. The first thing they saw was a body hanging from the portcullis. Above the body, a sign: *Do Not Rebel.* The butcher's corpse swayed. Reficul's curse activated — bullywug this time — and they pressed on.

Meanwhile, Inquizo had gone ahead of everyone and gone invisible.

His recon of the palace was thorough and grim. A prison building held mostly women, birds, and mermaids — the populations this king had decided were threats or tools or both. Bodies on both portcullises. Pmurt's bedroom: empty. The king was not sleeping. Inquizo tracked what he needed, withdrew, and reported back.

The party gathered. They bought supplies. George bought a war hammer — finally, a real one, even if it wasn't *his*. They disguised Mira (human, relatively unnoticed in a city that surveils the unusual), and they went in.

Their stealth results going through the palace: 28, 24, 14, 8, and George-3. A persuasion roll of 17 got them past the first set of guards. The kitchen was the problem. A chef discovered them. The chef did not survive the discovery. The transition from infiltration to open combat had begun.

What followed in the throne room was one of the biggest fights of the campaign.

King Pmurt — English breakfast in hand, Nightmare entities as his patron saints, and Numgis's absent blessing behind him — fought the party across two phases. Phase one: conventional king-with-guards combat. He cast Fear; George failed the save and fled the room. He threw his breakfast around with bizarre effectiveness. Reficul hit him with Dissonant Whispers for 32 damage — a single spell, a devastating number. Halcyra applied Searing Smite and lit him on fire. Inquizo pulled the chandelier lever. George came back through the door and used Thunder Wave. Then used it again. The second cast landed in the middle of the wave of guards Pmurt had summoned — approximately twenty of them — and the room went quiet.

Twenty palace guards died in a single cast. George did not comment on this. He moved on.

Phase two: Pmurt tapped his shoulder. Earlier in the fight. The party had not known what that meant. He summoned a phantasmal spectator — a CR5 construct of psychic malevolence — and Reficul took it down with Starry Wisp. But the damage was done. They had spent their resources. Pmurt was near death. And then the contingency activated.

He was gone.

The throne room was quiet. Pmurt was alive — badly wounded, somewhere he hadn't been a moment ago, and furious. And partially castrated. Because Mira had gotten there with her sickle first.

The pact weapon had manifested during the fight — a sickle, no different in shape from the one Grungrak had given Persephone, but hers, and from a patron she had never consciously agreed to serve. She is an unknowing warlock. Whatever entity is extending her those abilities, it was present in the throne room. After the Zone of Truth interrogation — Pmurt compelled, briefly, to answer honestly about what he knew — Mira acted with the sickle, and Pmurt escaped mid-consequence.

He took something with him when he left. Left something behind when he went.

The party stood in a ruined throne room. Twenty dead guards. A spectator's goop. A chandelier on the floor. Numgis already gone north for reasons no one had explained. Pmurt alive and running.

The DM said: level up to 6.

The session ended there — in the wreckage of a fight that wasn't finished, in a city that was still figuring out whether its king had just been dethroned or merely inconvenienced, in a story that had just grown several new threads.

### Session 12 — The Shadow Selves

The session began in the throne room wreckage and ended on a couch with everyone crying.

The party was still in the island city, still in the ruins of a fight that had escaped them when the king poofed mid-castration. They investigated for Numgis's room and found it pointed to by a neon sign — Vegas-style merch lights all around the throne room, because of course there were — and ended up in the prison. Most of the cells were already open. A tube had been hammered through the glass fish tank at the back, and the mermaids and birds had apparently already left. Only three men remained in the last occupied cell.

One of them had eyes that shifted from brilliant gold to silver. He described being on his farm when blue lightning tore the sky, paper tearing across reality, the sound of a machine. He had been in this prison cell since. Mira did the math and confirmed there was nothing in the historical record about biomatter being merged in the Shattering — but there were whispers about gods appearing differently than they used to, or not answering at all. The farmer's shifting eyes were not his own. Something was layered onto him when the worlds collided. He absorbed this information, said he needed to find someone, and jumped through the fish tank and up the tube into the sea.

Outside on the beach, the party met the Liberators.

They were six men in pastel colors, cheerful and confusing. Their spokesperson was a man named Elton — five foot six, a natural talker, apparently not troubled by the fact that he had arrived in this world by springing from the pages of a book. A seven-foot jovial old man had sent them, they said. Gave them a symbol and told them there were people in danger. This was, the party confirmed to each other in quiet horror, the same figure who had been adjacent to every major mystery in their own journey. The Liberators had already dealt with the birds — they had gone to the eagle king's nest and found a man with wire glasses there, already setting up some kind of system related to sleep. They left satisfied. They had not known what they were looking at.

The party knew.

The wire-glasses man was Numgis. He had been at the Bird Kingdom — at Voxus — two days ago. He was already in position.

The party departed the island city after that. They freed the remaining guards from Pmurt's chain of command, uncovered the falcon face on the gold-topped bird statue (Mira confirmed it was historically significant; she couldn't name it), and left. The Liberators went separately, toward the fish. The party went north.

Voxus was the Bird Kingdom — built like the Rito from an old story, layered into the mountainside, enormous and structured and ancient in ways the party was not equipped to fully appreciate on a quick visit. They met Gunj. He was an owl-adjacent figure, a shadow therapist, someone who worked with the nightmare side of consciousness as a professional practice. He had something to tell them, but first he had a thing he needed to try.

He walked them through breathing exercises. He faded them to black. And then each of them found themselves somewhere that was not the room they were sitting in.

Mira found herself in a graveyard with a better-dressed version of herself, already doing fine, holding her tethered by something physical. *You could have healed him,* the shadow said. *You don't have the guts.* Mira did not have guts and did not claim to. She had study, and she had intent, and she had the sense that her limits were hers to name. The shadow called this weakness. Mira called it honesty.

Inquizo found it snowing. He could smell someone's breakfast. A voice called his name from a doorway and he ran from it immediately, full sprint, before anyone had finished speaking. His shadow copied every movement effortlessly. The shadow showed him his sister's silhouette in the path ahead and he ran through it. *You fail her again.* He found an alley. His shadow helped him hide. Chains wrapped his arms, his neck. A figure of his brother appeared. The shadow said *we need to protect him* and Inquizo covered what would have been his ears, if he had them, and screamed *what do you want?* The shadow said it wanted what he wanted. He said he had already tried and made things worse. He curled into a ball. The chains became bracelets. He woke up saying *I'm sorry* over and over.

Halcyra was flying — the best flying of her life, sunlight on her wings, utterly free — and an older version of her was beside her asking why she had ever stopped. She said her mother told her to hide. The older Halcyra's face became her mother's and screamed: *Why have you left me?* She crashed into her own farmhouse. Her mother was in front of her. There was also a voice — booming, familiar, kind, coming from the sky and calling her name. The two tugs ran in opposite directions: her mother, begging for one more minute, saying she had something related to her father to give her; and the voice overhead, louder each time, saying *the worlds are at stake.* She went to her mother, whispered *I promise I do love you,* and walked out the door toward the sky.

Reficul was shown a small cottage. Children laughing. His name called lovingly. No contract, no debt, just a life. His shadow had lived it instead of him. *Would you like to hear something about life?* Yes. Everything I can't have. He said the shadow had regrets; the shadow was proud of not making the mistake. He pointed out that the shadow had also never learned anything. *Life is always better when it isn't worse,* he said. The shadow tried arguments about peace and freedom and violence. Reficul kept redirecting. Eventually the shadow said it was looking for a deity who could change form, and Reficul asked *what form are you?* and the shadow said *I am but a shout.* And then it merged with him, becoming a tattoo of darkness across his left arm, because that is the only way to conclude an argument with Reficul — to give up and become part of him.

And then Mira walked into a dragon.

She had been talking to her father's image in the dream when her shadow grew. Green veins became wings. Her head shifted into a dragon's head. Before her stood a blue dragon with green veins — her patron, her dark power, the thing that had been speaking through her in a masculine voice and manifesting the sickle she still barely understood.

Inside the dragon, the DM had expected denial. Mira does not deny; Mira deflects. She walked into it and found Grungrak inside.

The dragon told her she was the one behind the deaths. She asked it what the other Mira — the one in the mirror — had done differently when told to run. *She saved.* Black tendrils came from Mira and held the dragon still. She dealt the final blow. She found the cord — physical, real — and severed it.

And Grungrak became physical. Not a vision. Not a memory. Real. He hugged her and said *thank you for letting me be me* and slowly faded.

The table cried.

After Grungrak faded, her shadow shrank to a small frightened girl. *What am I when they all leave?* You are you. *Am I enough?* What is enough for. Then the girl gave her what she named as her father's final gift — a lightning bolt necklace — and faded.

Mira had learned something. Her father had sold his soul to save her when she was five and first getting sick. The contract read: *my soul for hers.* The patron, the dragon, the masculine voice, the sickle — all of it was downstream of that transaction. She had been purchased. Her father was gone somewhere because of her. And the necklace around her neck was from him, passed through the smallest version of herself, in a dream that was also somehow true.

Gunj, after they woke, gave them tissue boxes and one more piece of information.

He had been on his way to unseal the God of Dreams. Part of the unsealing mechanism was in the next town. The God of Dreams was sealed by her own nightmare — her own shadow — and Numgis was working with that nightmare, for it, deliberately. Gunj believed this had to be stopped. He could not help much. He had duties and a chief who believed the town might need to be destroyed. He would stall as long as he could.

*It's one thing to kill people,* he said. *It's another to torment them in their dreams.*

The party sat on the couch — Inquizo still muttering, George still weeping through his smile, Halcyra holding a bow, Mira holding a necklace, Reficul pretending to be asleep — and understood that the next thing was going to be harder than this one.

## Connecting the Threads

Two god-statues were stolen from the underwater city: "Poppa Poseidon" and the "God of the Seas." The island tribe has at least one — they defaced it and put their leader's face on it. Ariel hired the pirate captain to retrieve them. Ariel is now dead.

Halcyra doesn't know she's the child of prophecy or Apollo's daughter. Everything about her — the wings, the paladin class, the divine blood — is pointing at a destiny she hasn't been told about. The underwater city's war with "bird folk" is not a coincidence in this context. Halcyra spent all of Session 2 hiding her wings. The world keeps trying to push her away from her own identity while her identity is quietly the most important thing in the room.

Something brought Mira back. The question isn't whether this matters — it clearly does, the field proved that — the question is *who* brought her back and *why*. Options on the table:

The pit area's white strings may be connected to whatever is anchoring her here, or whatever is trying to pull her back. Something was invited. Something was asked to return. The party killed one construct. The source is still out there, and the caravan campsite in the woods hasn’t been investigated yet.

### Session 13 — The Ship Has a Name

The party woke on Gunj's couch still raw from Session 12. Everyone had met their shadow. Everyone had received something they hadn't asked for and weren't sure what to do with. The world needed them to move anyway.

Before they were fully awake, they dreamed.

Halcyra asked him about the prophecy she has been carrying since she was fifteen. He said it wasn't his realm. When she pushed, he named the god whose realm it was: *He's an asshole. It's Apollo.* He added, almost as an aside, that there are beings above even Apollo. He didn't know who. He left in a gust of wind before anyone could follow up.

Apollo. The god of prophecy. The same voice that called Halcyra's name three times in her shadow dream and said the worlds are at stake. ### Session 14 — The Red Sky

The ship had a dog in it.

This is the detail that set the tone for the first half of the session: a wizard pug in a star-and-moon robe and an enormous hat, with wanted posters for himself, sitting in the crew quarters of a stolen airship as if he had always lived there. Telepathic. Calm. He stole George's manual immediately and regarded the party with the air of someone who has seen considerably worse.

His name is Gbot'n. He has a restaurant across the storm — the spiral storm the ship briefly tried to fly into after its reboot, the one with a screaming face in the cloud formation if you looked at it long enough. How long he has been aboard, and why, are not questions he chose to answer clearly.

George had found the manual before Gbot'n took it: a ring-bound volume with sections on engineering, the server room, weapons, and a floor plan. He also located a typewriter near the navigation helm and used it to re-register the party as the ship's crew, replacing "King Mert" in the AI's recognition. While doing this, he was the only one to hear the full reboot announcement — nat 20 Wisdom, present for every word.

*"Reboot completed. Memory erased, wind collection restored, heart beating, destination status unknown, origin remembered. When the child breathes the vessel remembers the womb. Return the heart, return the breath, return the wandering daughter. Course corrected for home."*

The ship has a home. It knows where it is from.

---

The navigation circle required four medallions to set course: sun, moon, star, and gear. Getting each one was its own small story.

The moon came from a needle lock labeled "PSI 16" — Gbot'n barked at it once to activate the sequence, but the actual mechanism needed a human breath. Mira blew into it (roll 18 vs DC 17, cleared by one), and the moon pendant dropped free. She went to the navigation circle and set destination: **Bermuda Foundation**. The Doctor's base. She did not ask the party's opinion about this.

The star was in the sail rigging. Inquizo pulled the sail pins to see what would happen, the mechanism swung, and the star medallion hit Inquizo directly in the face before falling to the deck. He recovered. He kept the bruise to himself.

The sun required talking to whatever was inside the second glass case.

Halcyra had found that case in Session 13, tripping on a tapestry that showed a sun, rays, wings, and sheep. She hadn't opened it. Nobody had. Now, with the navigation puzzle requiring a sun medallion and the case sitting right there, someone had to.

She rolled a nat 20.

What was inside: a burned humanoid figure with many eyes, connected to the ship by wires. It could talk. It had opinions. It was, in the phrasing of someone who has assessed many difficult situations, extremely sassy. Halcyra spoke to it with patience and respect, and after a genuine conversation, the creature handed over the sun pendant — its own medallion — and let it go into the circle.

Three medallions placed. The gear medallion has not been found.

---

Before they arrived at the Doctor's base, they slept.

Gbot'n cooked. Not scraps — a full feast, the kind that gives your whole body 10 temporary hitpoints and your soul a moment to breathe. Dex was apparently awake for all of it. Not by choice. By the end of the night he had dark circles under his eyes and the expression of a man who had been outpaced by a dog for eight hours.

The party rested. The grandfather clock below deck ticked. Starglass breathed in her case. The ship moved north.

---

Mira saw Bermuda Foundation first.

She was in the kitchen when the city came into view through the window. Blood-red sky. Every building on fire. Desolate streets, no movement. She didn't shout. She looked at it for a moment — took it in the way she takes in terrible things, quietly and completely — and then told the others.

They had arrived at the Doctor's base. The Doctor's base was burning.

Before anyone could process what to do about a burning city, a figure appeared on the ground below: **Lieutenant Galveston**. Six and a half feet tall, red braid, smoke gauntlets on both hands. He had brought a red carpet. He had also brought four dobermans on leashes. He was smiling.

*"You work for the Doctor, correct?"*

George said yes. George said this with complete conviction and absolutely no plan. The crowd of onlookers who had gathered (there was a crowd despite the burning buildings) cheered. Galveston welcomed the party warmly and said the Doctor would return shortly — he'd just stepped out. The ship was invited to fly up to the Doctor's office to wait.

They flew up. And then Galveston transformed.

---

The transformation was not clean. Whatever Galveston had been was replaced by something insectoid — his body reshaping, the dobermans becoming hellhounds in the same moment. The combat was immediate.

Inquizo hid first. Stealth 24. He found a position and held it until the right moment, then deployed his Chains of Eternity — launching them at Galveston and restoring the old Marley logic: restrained, but so is he. He took 14 damage in that exchange without flinching about it.

George used Plant Growth to turn the deck into a slow-motion nightmare for the hellhounds moving through it. Dense, tangled, uncooperative greenery in every gap they tried to cross.

Mira cast Hold Person. Galveston saved with an 18. She didn't hesitate — she shifted to Mind Spike and landed it, and something fell from Galveston's eye when it hit. An insect. Small, dark, dislodged by the psychic strike. Whatever Galveston had been possessed by lost its grip in that eye-socket for a moment, and the insect tumbled free.

*"A different man underneath his skin,"* Starglass had said about the Doctor.

Halcyra ended it. Bow of Dusk, Snare Strike — the Doctor's lieutenant went down with his arm blown off. When the red sky cleared, Galveston and his four Dobermans walked off the ship as if nothing had happened. Not confirmed dead.

The hellhounds fell. The deck was quiet except for George's Plant Growth settling into the floor planks.

---

Then George went below.

He had heard something during the fight — or felt it. Something wrong in the ship's bones. He left the deck and went down to the clock room.

The grandfather clock was spinning wildly. Not ticking. Spinning. The room around it was **rusting** in real time — metal surfaces oxidizing, flaking, the room aging visibly. And Starglass, in her glass case, was breathing hard. Short, labored. Her pipes straining. The room around her decaying as if time were moving too fast.

He came back up to tell the party.

Inquizo went below.

He looked at the clock. He looked at the spinning hands. He reached out, grabbed them, and turned them counter-clockwise.

Time reversed. The scene returned to before the combat — before the red sky, before Galveston's transformation, before the hellhounds. From Galveston's perspective and the Dobermans', nothing had happened. They walked off the ship. The party kept their memories: the insect in Galveston's eye, the arm blown off, the hellhounds, all of it. Physical traces of the encounter may still have been present — but the Doctor's lieutenant walked off a ship where, officially, nothing had occurred.

---

The clock can reverse time. What it costs, how far it can go, and what Inquizo now is to a ship that uses its living core to breathe — these are the questions that will follow him into the next session.

The Doctor was returning shortly. His base is burning. His lieutenant doesn't know why.

## Session 15 — The Gentle Descent

The session the DM called a beach episode was not a beach episode.

The party disembarked at Bermuda Foundation's Los Alamos district and immediately found a girl in a yellow dress with fifteen orphaned children, a locked study that hadn't been opened in years, and a fog-filled room with a slime creature cycling through forms. The slime became a skeleton. The skeleton became THETA — a former judge, naked, bewildered, and profoundly relieved that someone had finally arrived.

Under Zone of Truth, THETA told them what he'd been told to protect: a child named Ariel, sometimes called Bylo. He told them who had locked him in: a metal individual, likely one of Millaphera's automatons. He told them what the second half of Dr. Larkin's notebook said.

The expedition that created the automatons wasn't an accident. They killed Millaphera's family. The automatons are grief, shaped by someone who understood how to give grief a body.

A drawer in the study held undelivered letters between Constance and her brother Iskander. Neither of them had ever received the other's words. Constance didn't say anything when she understood what she was looking at. She just held them.

Then they went to a theme park.

Canary Park is everything you'd expect a humanitarian amusement park built by a family called Tempest to be: birds everywhere, a Ferris wheel half underground, a mine ride called the Gentle Descent, and an old man in the ticket booth who put on googly-eye glasses and told them the deeper you go, the less the world belongs to you. He said it like a park slogan. It may have been.

Before the park, the party passed through The Eustace — a community of people who can only be nourished by bread. They grow vegetables they can taste but not absorb, and grain to make the bread that keeps them alive. Among them was Alia, daughter of Arcus the lighthouse keeper, who had been brought here when her island became too dangerous. She was building a boat to go back. She gave Halcyra a set of wooden pan pipes she had made herself. When Halcyra played them, three people appeared — all of them called themselves Aspen.

At the unnamed tavern, George challenged a man in Gambison armor named Anson to three whiskey-bottle contests in a row. He won all three. He used Cutting Words to win the wrestling match too. He earned four hundred gold and was only slightly drunk. Gbot'n appeared on a table, declared economic war on the restaurant, and floated out the window before anyone could finish a sentence.

The mine ride separated the carts. The guide said "what a pity, good luck" and went somewhere safe. George threw up. The vomit arc hit a stranger. Liz dodged.

At the bottom of the track: a glowing blue clock. And Stuart.

Stuart the green sheep from the Session 5 cult was standing in the mountain with wings growing from his wool and fire coming from his mouth, possessed by a scarab attached to his skull by what appeared to be organic tendons. The same mechanism as Galveston's eye insect. The same pattern as the Doctor's "different man underneath his skin." Whatever the Doctor does to people, it also works on sheep.

Mira stopped the clock's second hand with Mage Hand. Plant growth jammed its gears; the face fell off; a cuckoo emerged. Inquizo grabbed the hour hand and was spun in circles, functioning as a brake. Two skeletons — neutral once Stuart was freed — helped stop the clock completely. Halcyra cut the scarab off Stuart's skull. He reverted to sheep form and vanished. The skeletons dissolved.

A woman dangling off a railing — metal armor, orange eyes, painter's cap, locket — sprouted wings and flew them out of the cave. She said she had a safehouse in this very district. She was running from people unhappy she was adopted. Then she ran.

Time outside the mine had been moving at a different pace. The party spent ten minutes inside. Liz waited two hours.

The session ended with the group walking back toward Paradise Manor and the winged woman's safehouse. The Doctor's district surrounds them. Another clock has appeared. Another possession mechanism confirmed. Another person with a story they haven't finished telling.

## Session 16 — The World Untrue

Everyone was asleep when it started.

The party was on the road through Eustace when the sleeping sickness found them. One by one they went under — all except Inquizo, who rolled a natural twenty and stood alone among collapsed bodies while a cuckoo bird landed on Mira's head. Mira, half-dreaming, tried to remove it. She rolled a natural one. She knocked herself out.

There were elephant-like creatures on the road. There was an albino man in a dreamcatcher vest. When the party woke, they were in a limousine wagon — wrists cuffed, cargo, moving.

George woke up first and smashed the albino man's wrist. The albino man screamed. Inquizo had stayed invisible the whole time. Halcyra used Enthralling Strike — vines tangled up her captor and she fell paralyzed. Halcyra shot Mira's captor to free her. Mira had been awake the whole time, pretending not to be. Inside the wagon, Mira cast Hold Person on the guard about to cuff her — the guard rolled a 2 and went down. Halcyra cuffed her. She didn't have a key. She took two more pairs anyway.

The woman in charge of the wagon was named Lila Monger. She carried a barbed-wire spear and had views about truth that she delivered with the sincerity of someone who had given this speech before. She was a loyalist of Amasia, which is apparently a real faction and not something the party had previously had cause to think about. She released them after they agreed to hear her out. She gave them sausages and eggs. She said the organization they'd just been fighting — Gore Harlow's operation — had been treating the garrison like a private resource. She wanted them to understand the difference between what the COG was supposed to be and what it had become. Then she went her own way.

Constance told them about Eskander on the train.

She said Gore Harlow had a son named Eskander. Eskander had been working inside the organization until he wasn't — until he'd decided Constance was worth more to him than the family business. The train crossed a long bridge over nothing.

The party did not get to introduce themselves.

The sky was wrong. The capital's clock tower was broken in a way that spread across the city like weather. The woman they'd been looking for was within reach. Session 16 ended there.
