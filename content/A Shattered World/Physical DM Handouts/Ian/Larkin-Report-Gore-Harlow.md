# Larkin's Observational Report — Gore Harlow

*Luminary of Mechanical Regulation, Royal Council of Aerolon. Filed under Internal Observation Records. Year 1718.*

---

**Subject:** Gore Harlow
**Origin:** Amasian immigrant (unverified coastal registry; probable western Amasian lineage)
**Location of Interest:** Aerofort District and surrounding quarters

---

It has been my observation that certain individuals, though outwardly unremarkable within the civic body, exert disproportionate influence upon those within their personal orbit. Gore Harlow appears to be one such case.

Harlow is an immigrant of Amasian origin whose present livelihood is connected to the establishment colloquially referred to as the **House of Long Sentences** — a business whose operations occupy a grey margin between hospitality and vice. Reports place him there frequently in a supervisory or managerial role, though the precise nature of his authority remains unclear.

From collected testimonies and interviews, Harlow demonstrates a behavioral pattern that may be described as **performative dominance**. He presents himself with deliberate confidence and theatrical masculinity, often invoking rigid expectations of what he considers proper male conduct. In social interactions he favors declarative statements about strength, provision, and control, usually delivered as instructive remarks toward younger men within his household.

---

Particularly noteworthy is his relationship with two youths under his care.

The first, **Tripper**, his biological son, receives material provisions at a level that exceeds the average household of the district. Clothing, tools, and small luxuries are regularly provided. However, the testimonies of educators and neighbors suggest a parallel absence of emotional engagement. Harlow appears to substitute gifts for parental presence, leaving the boy socially unanchored.

The second youth, **Iskander**, his stepson, receives markedly different treatment. Witness accounts describe Harlow interacting with him less as a child and more as a subject of instruction. Encouragement is rare; instead he appears to impose a doctrine of stoicism and analytic restraint. Several recorded remarks attributed to Harlow describe the boy in aspirational terms — "an example of what a man should be." The phrasing suggests that Harlow views Iskander not primarily as family, but as a project of ideological refinement.

The household dynamic is therefore asymmetrical: one child materially indulged yet emotionally unattended, the other intellectually molded yet socially distanced. Such arrangements often yield long-term behavioral distortions in both parties.

---

In contrast to these otherwise rigid attitudes, Harlow exhibits an uncharacteristically sentimental disposition toward a woman identified as **Mari**. Informants consistently recount statements from him describing her as central to his personal motivations. He has reportedly expressed the desire to construct for her a **"living paradise"** — a phrase repeated often enough that it may be genuine rather than rhetorical.

*(Note: Mari Hokami is believed to be Iskander's biological mother — see Iskander report.)*

---

Harlow bears a visible tattoo of a **baku** along the length of his right arm. Within Amasian symbolic traditions the baku is associated with the consumption of nightmares and the regulation of dreams. While tattoos of this nature are not uncommon among Amasian migrants, the placement and scale suggest deliberate self-identification with the motif.

Rumors persist that Harlow carries or has carried a **Tagha blade**, a weapon of distinctive Amasian manufacture. No direct confirmation has been obtained.

---

Of greater analytical interest is Harlow's **recurring proximity to several recent incidents of the so-called sleeping sickness**. These correlations may be coincidence; the affected neighborhoods overlap with areas he is known to frequent. However, the pattern has occurred often enough to merit continued observation.

At present, there is insufficient evidence to categorize Harlow as a direct instigator of civic disturbance. He is, however, an individual whose personal philosophy, influence over impressionable youths, and unexplained proximity to emerging phenomena warrant continued monitoring.

Further reports pending.

*— Dr. Morgenstern Alistair Larkin*
*Luminary of the Royal Council*
