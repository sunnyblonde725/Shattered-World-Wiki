# Larkin's Notebook — The Deep Entity's Writing

*Written in bold text directly into Larkin's journal. The entity writes as though overwriting his entries. Refers to Larkin as "mr math" and "the Doctor" — or signs itself as "the doctor."*

---

**There are pages in this journal that stink of rhetoric and doubt. You have little use for them.**

**You, who wrote them, believe the world to be a machine that simply needs patience and intellect to reveal its secrets. You believe discovery is clean, and knowledge is earned through careful observation and tidy conclusions.**

**You will never understand what truly moves the world. Your curiosity? No.**

**Betrayal.**

---

**I write now because the girl in the glass remembers too much. How did she avoid the veil? Was it a malfunction? She was not meant to remember.**

**When we first opened her chest and placed the breathing pipes within her lungs, the intention was practical. The vessel required a heart that could withstand pressures no ordinary flesh could survive. Her body, already half-alchemical, half-organic, offered the perfect compromise between machine and organism. She breathes for the ship. The ship breathes for her. You're so so smart mr math.**

**A tidy equation, don't you think? A shame you cannot see the child still dreams. She dreams of her mother. She speaks of her with reverence, as though the creature that made her were something sacred rather than something full of shame.**

---

**Millaphera. Even writing the name puts rust in my throat. We taught the language of creation to her long before the surface dwellers learned to stack stone into towers. The runes the girl carries across her body are not decorations or the childish symbols my people in that bronze city carve into murals when they think no one is watching.**

**They are my instructions. Greetings, yes. Farewells, yes. But also the first syllables of life itself. Millaphera should've known better. Like all creatures who are granted power beyond their station, she mistook instruction for permission.**

**She made constructs. Things, and calls them children.**

**Little fragments of herself wander through a world they were never meant to inherit. The girl calls herself a piece of her mother's heart. A sentimental phrase. Incorrect, of course. She is not a heart.**

**Now remember, boy, the Deep does not forget its traitors.**

---

**When we arrived with your companions and spoke to Millaphera, I gave her the opportunity to correct her error of fecundity. The laws beneath the world are older than the wind above it. They are patient, but they are not forgiving. We must have frightened her. She refused our offer. She ran from your men.**

**The child was left behind. Children are excellent teachers of discipline.**

**Millaphera does not seek revenge for what has happened to her daughter. This is perhaps the most disappointing element of the entire affair. She accepts the punishment as though she believes suffering will somehow restore balance. I thought Millaphera was a better mother than that, but I suppose she deserved her place in the deep after all.**

**This effect will not restore anything, but it does serve a purpose. Every breath the girl takes through those pipes will be written as a sermon. Every pulse of air through the ship will be a reminder of what happens when one abandons their place in the order of things.**

**This lesson will become what is beautiful about Millaphera's daughter. The memory of her, etched into this moment in time forever will serve to build many's perception of perfection. Just another cog in the wheel to you, eh, Doctor?**

---

**The surface dwellers are far worse than my children. The city of bronze especially.**

**Their towers stretch into the sky as though height itself were proof of their intellect. They capture wind, chain lightning, and grind the breath of the world into engines. They celebrate their cleverness with parades and orchestras while the foundations of their civilization rot with arrogance. To think.**

**They believe themselves untouchable. They believe the Deep does not notice their oppressive curiosity. Your people believe progress is a virtue rather than a disease.**

**It is almost charming.**

**At least my girl is useful. She was built on a fount of truth. Your entire lives are made on a foundation of lies, the likes of which you all believe. What a pity.**

**The ship required a living heart, and the girl provided it. The vessel changed hands exactly as the memory serves. A crown was exchanged for rapport, bargains were struck, and the surface world continued turning its gears without realizing what had been placed among them. Just for your information. That happened. Were you asleep when it did? Poor mr math.**

---

**I see a memory. Another you don't have. They're making their way to our home.**

**North. The story, like weather, clouds, moving from afar.**

**The wind has already begun to whisper to the children who still remember how to listen. One of them, the boy she calls Haki, has already taken the first step. He walks beside my small prophet dressed in white, glowing like a candle held against a storm. You'll never find her. Not even your boy could.**

**The voice that leads them will arrive on the wind, telling them that danger follows. Even if you could find them, they'd fear you. Just like Millaphera did.**

---

**Millaphera's sister, at least, understands what must happen.**

**While one sibling hides behind remorse and misconstrued mercy, the other prepares herself for the necessary work ahead. She does not speak of forgiveness. She does not waste breath mourning the past.**

**She breathes war, similar to your fellow luminary.**

**When the time comes, the bronze city will not fall with the quiet dignity its scholars would prefer. Its towers will crack open like old bone. Its canals will fill with something thicker than water.**

**Bronze is a poor material for a civilization.**

**Bone and blood endure far longer.**

---

**As for the girl, I sometimes wonder whether you realize how close she is to freedom. The mechanical cage that keeps her alive can be dismantled. Her consciousness can be unwound from the vessel and placed somewhere far more appropriate. She might even be returned to the Deep where she belongs.**

**Or perhaps she will remain exactly where she is. Your kindness is foolish. Your hesitation, built on politry will be the undoing of many.**

**A heart beats inside a machine. The material components are much too far off, and you call yourself a doctor.**

**A warning will carry across the sky. Five. Now it all depends on what the world chooses to do next.**

**You believe you will soon be rid of me. Conviction will not save you. You have always been very confident in your conclusions. We will see whether conviction survives the moment when the knife is in your own hand. For now, let your foolish experiment continue.**

**The city of bronze keeps breathing.**

**That will not last forever.**

**— the doctor**

## Notes

- The entity writes **in bold** directly into Larkin's journal — the text is overwriting his own entries
- It signs itself **"the doctor"** — either it has adopted Larkin's title, or it refers to itself this way, or it has become the Doctor
- **Haki** — the boy walking beside the glowing prophetess in white. This is Starglass's brother, referenced in Session 13. The prophetess is the one Starglass said her brother is protecting.
- **Millaphera's sister** — confirmed. She exists, she "breathes war," she is preparing for violence against the bronze city. Millaphera mourns; the sister does not.
- **"A warning will carry across the sky. Five."** — unclear what "Five" means. A countdown? Five warnings? Five somethings?
- **"The bronze city"** — the entity hates this civilization above all others. Its towers, its engineers, its progress. This is likely Aerolon.
- **The runes on Starglass's body** are the Deep entity's language — "my instructions... the first syllables of life itself." Millaphera was taught them and used them to make her constructs.
- **"The vessel changed hands exactly as the memory serves. A crown was exchanged for rapport"** — Pmurt's crown? The airship changed ownership through some deal involving a crown.
