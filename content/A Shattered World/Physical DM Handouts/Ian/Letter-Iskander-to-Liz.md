# Letter — Iskander to Liz

*Undated. Written during or after Cadet training.*

---

Dearest Elizabeth,

I will endure the embarrassment of repetition if it increases, even by some infinitesimal margin, the likelihood that one of these letters should find its way into your hands. So I begin, as I always have:

How are you?

It is a deceptively small question, yet I find it contains the whole of my concern. I have not heard from you, nor from any of the others, and in that silence the mind becomes an unruly instrument, playing out tragedies where none may yet exist. I trust, stubbornly and perhaps foolishly, that you are safe.

Time has not waited for either of us. I imagine you taller, sharper, perhaps sterner than when last I saw you. It would be unfair if you alone had changed — so I will admit that I have as well. Or at least, I am trying to, and I shall remain welcome to you.

I have entered the Cadet ranks. By the time this letter is read, if it is read, I will likely already be in transit toward my first assignment. The work is demanding. It requires a certain discipline of thought that I suspect Larkin would approve of, though I cannot claim to meet his standards without reservation.

I have, however, made allies. Competent ones. You would like some of them, I think. Though I remain convinced none possess your particular talent for disregard.

Following my graduation, I have been granted certain considerations. Privileges, they call them. Hollow things, but useful nonetheless. I have already secured accommodations sufficient for more than just myself. When I return, I intend to find you. All of you. That is not a hope, Liz, it is a certainty I am in the process of constructing.

Tell me, if this reaches you: have you heard from Doctor Larkin? His absence is conspicuous if nothing else. Until I know more, I would ask — no, insist — that you remain where you are, insofar as you are able. Stability is preferable to wandering blind.

When next I write, I may be above you — quite literally. I have instruments now, lenses and looking glasses that make a mockery of distance. Should it become necessary, I will turn them upon the earth itself. I will trace every road, every roof, every shaded corner until I find you.

You are not lost to me. Not yet. Not ever.

So remain.
I need you to.

Wherever you are.

*Steadfast and yours,*
*Iskander*

---

## Notes

- Iskander is in the **Cadet ranks / military** — had already enlisted when this was written
- He refers to Larkin as "Doctor Larkin" and speaks of him with clear respect and concern at his absence
- "Instruments now, lenses and looking glasses" — he has surveillance capability; he is trying to locate Liz
- "Accommodations sufficient for more than just myself" — he planned to bring the children home
- This letter was also never delivered — found alongside Liz's undelivered letters in the study
