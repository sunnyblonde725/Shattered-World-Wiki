# Larkin's Observational Report — Iskander

*Luminary of Mechanical Regulation, Royal Council of Aerolon. Filed within Civic Observation Dossiers — Restricted Access. Year 1718.*

---

**Subject:** Iskander
**Origin:** Aerofort District
**Age:** Adolescent (exact year unverified due to incomplete registry records)

---

Among the numerous youths emerging from the Aerofort following the recent unrest, one individual has repeatedly drawn the attention of both civic authorities and military observers. The boy known as Iskander presents a profile worthy of continued documentation.

Physically, the subject appears unremarkable at first inspection. His frame is slight and compact rather than imposing, the build typical of individuals raised within working-class districts where endurance replaces luxury. However, repeated reports from instructors and witnesses indicate that his apparent fragility conceals a notable degree of physical capability. Reaction speed, balance, and coordination exceed expected parameters for his age. More striking still is his apparent tolerance for physical strain; injuries that would ordinarily limit a youth's activity seem to slow him only briefly.

The boy's body bears numerous scars, many predating his adolescence. Their distribution — across the forearms, shoulders, and back — suggests repeated exposure to physical discipline rather than accidental injury. While no formal accusation has been levied, the pattern aligns with behavioral reports concerning his stepfather, Gore Harlow, whose documented temperament leaves the possibility of harsh domestic enforcement difficult to dismiss.

---

Iskander's upbringing occurred almost entirely within the House of Long Sentences, the Aerofort establishment in which Harlow maintains authority. Records from the district indicate that the boy was born there, and that much of his early development took place under the informal supervision of two women associated with the household: Lila Monger and Mari Hokami.

**Mari Hokami is believed to have been his biological mother.**

Her death, attributed to the lingering effects of the sleeping sickness that has troubled the lower districts, occurred while the boy was absent from the immediate vicinity. Witness accounts describe the child as having shown little outward emotional response upon learning of the event, though the reliability of such observations remains uncertain. Children raised in environments of constant hardship often conceal grief in ways that resemble indifference.

---

During his early adolescence, Iskander appears to have been assigned responsibilities within the house that extended beyond those typical of his age. Notably, he was tasked with supervising a number of orphaned children left in the district by soldiers of the national military whose prior associations with the establishment were later erased from official record.

It is therefore unsurprising that the subject exhibits behavioral traits associated with early assumption of authority: measured speech, controlled emotional display, and a tendency toward analytical observation of social dynamics.

Physically he is identifiable by **black hair, dark brown eyes, and pronounced shadows beneath the eyes** — a common feature among the laboring population of Aerofort.

---

The event that most significantly altered Iskander's trajectory occurred during the Amasian uprising in the district. Accounts from several municipal agents indicate that the boy played a role, however modest, in assisting local efforts to stabilize the unrest. The outcome appears to have earned him quiet admiration among certain administrative circles.

In my own estimation, the episode revealed two characteristics of considerable importance: courage in the presence of civic disorder, and a willingness to act independently of adult direction when circumstances require.

For these reasons I have not hesitated to describe him, privately, as a potential asset to the nation.

Following his displacement from the Aerofort, the boy made his ambitions clear. He wishes to enlist in the National Military.

---

However, the subject's case is complicated by an observation I have hesitated to formalize until now.

Certain aspects of the boy's physical features and temperament suggest the possibility, however speculative, of **a genetic connection to Commander Kardos, Luminary of the National Military.**

This suspicion did not originate with me.

During a council banquet marking the recent turning of the year, the Commander himself shared with me, in confidence and under the influence of celebratory drink, a rather unorthodox personal theory. Kardos expressed the belief that his own genetic composition represented a particularly successful convergence of physical and cognitive traits, and that such qualities ought, in his view, to be more widely disseminated among the population.

The comment was delivered with sufficient levity that it might easily be dismissed as drunken exaggeration. Yet the possibility that he has acted upon this philosophy — whether consciously or otherwise — cannot be entirely disregarded.

At present there exists no evidence to suggest that Kardos has engaged in conduct beyond the bounds of his lawful marriage to **Esmeree Kardos**, and it would be irresponsible to imply impropriety without substantiation. Nevertheless, should proof of such behavior emerge, the political consequences would be substantial.

---

The Kardos household itself must also be considered in this equation. Their children are already figures of note within the capital.

The eldest son, **Fletcher**, has demonstrated remarkable aptitude in mechanical engineering and is presently studying under Allen Prothero, widely regarded as the most influential engineer of the present age.

The younger daughter, **Armageddon**, exhibits a different but equally intense form of ingenuity. She has inherited her mother's enthusiasm for experimental devices, many of which appear designed primarily for the production of controlled destruction.

Should the family become the subject of public scandal, both children would inevitably suffer consequences disproportionate to their own actions.

Thus discretion remains essential.

---

Returning to Iskander himself, I find my curiosity increasingly focused not merely on his abilities, but on his motivations. Does the boy pursue military service out of simple admiration for heroism? Or does he possess some awareness, conscious or otherwise, of the lineage that may connect him to the Commander he now seeks to serve?

There exists a more troubling possibility.

**It is conceivable that his ambition is not to serve Commander Kardos, but to confront him.**

Until such matters can be clarified, I intend to observe the boy closely during his academic instruction. Guidance may prove necessary. Youths shaped by hardship often carry within them both the capacity for extraordinary loyalty and the potential for equally extraordinary vengeance.

Iskander stands at a point where either path remains open.

I confess, however, that my own assessment of the boy remains optimistic.

He has endured more than most children of his age, yet the reports describing his conduct during the Aerofort unrest reveal restraint rather than cruelty. If given the opportunity to develop under proper mentorship, he may well become the sort of figure this nation requires in the years ahead: a soldier capable not merely of fighting for Aerolon, but of questioning the structures that govern it.

The boy need not prove his worth through suffering.

He has already endured enough.

In my judgment, the potential he carries within him may one day be sufficient not only to defend this nation, but to challenge those who have grown too comfortable in positions of inherited authority.

Whether that future arrives through loyalty… or through revolution… remains to be seen.

*— Dr. Morgenstern Alistair Larkin*
*Luminary of the Royal Council*
*Year 1718*
