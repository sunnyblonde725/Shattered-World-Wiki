## Recent Sessions

### Session 10 — The Thing That Eats

The party woke up at different hours and ate omelets. This sounds ordinary until you know that Reficul had been awake since 2 AM, failed his self-imposed push-up contract by two reps, and decided the correct response was to cook for everyone. It is a very specific kind of person who channels pre-dawn guilt into apologetic breakfast food. The omelets were apparently good.

Before setting out for the forest, they played a get-to-know-you card game — the spin-the-bottle kind, with questions. What came out of it was not small.

Reficul described the first time he picked up an ink and quill. His hands moved on their own, he said. He learned to write before he could speak. The writing was never legible to anyone else. He said this without drama, as if it were simply a thing that had happened, but everyone in the room knew what they were looking at: a child who was marked before he understood what marks meant. The same substance that now covers the walls of X-salent’s Victorian mansions and coats the bodies of dead animals in the woods apparently decided, long ago, to express itself through the hands of a changeling boy who couldn’t even talk yet.

Halcyra said she was 18. She said she left home at 18, over a disconnect with her mother about her father. Her father is Apollo. She didn’t say that part out loud — she doesn’t know it yet — but the shape of what she described was a girl who grew up knowing something was wrong with the way the sun worked, and a mother who couldn’t explain it, and a daughter who eventually stopped waiting for an explanation and left. She also said, quietly, that she believed George was part of the prophecy. That she had sworn to protect him. She did not say how long she had known this.

George talked about Grungrak. The real one. He said he was the most enthusiastic person he’d ever met. That he found George when he first arrived at the guild and decided they were going to be best friends before George had any say in the matter. That he was a talented storyteller. “And now it’s in our river,” he said, and stopped.

That’s the thing about George. He knows exactly who he’s a copy of. He carries it alongside the version of himself that survived, and most days he doesn’t put it down.

---

The caravan was blocking the gate when they left.

It was from Velodorn — a city no one had heard of until five months ago, which is when the world broke. The wagons were full of bedding and silk. The people were fine. The horses were fine. Every piece of cargo was coated, instantly and completely, in black inky goop the moment the wagons crossed the city threshold. Mira threw salt. It hardened like burnt caramel. Detect Magic read Transmutation school, tied to the gate boundary itself. The caravan driver was, reasonably, upset.

No one knew what to do about it, so the party went to the forest.

The trail was clearly made by someone who was frightened. Hardened candle wax pressed into the ground, each drip marking the frozen moment of someone moving too fast to hold a candle still. Broken branches at intervals, all at the same height — someone running, not walking, not looking back. Dropped torches, burned out where they fell.

The cave at the end of the trail was an old bear den, maybe 35 feet across. Inside: a dead man. Seven torches, all burned out. One still going. A journal, open on the ground.

They recognized him. He was the missing merchant. He had been missing for two weeks, and he had been here the whole time, dying in a cave surrounded by candles while something in the dark waited for him to stop moving.

They were still processing this when the thing came through the wall.

---

It moved the way ghosts move in stories — pressing through solid matter, insubstantial — but it wasn’t a ghost. It was a No-Face from a nightmare. It passed through the party at the entrance, walked directly to the body, and began eating it, and as it ate it became real. Visible. Present.

The DM described it as a fucked-up deer. Chronic wasting disease aesthetics, but worse: serpentine elements, a second head starting to form from the first, claws like a bear’s, fangs like hypodermic needles. Made entirely of the black inky substance. Not undead. Not a demon. Not a celestial. Halcyra’s Divine Sense came back blank on all three. It was something new, or something very old that the current classification systems hadn’t accounted for.

It hated light. When Mira hit it with Burning Hands, it screamed in multiple voices — some of them drowning, some of them wet in a way that suggested a mouth too full of teeth — and flailed desperately at a fire that wasn’t actually burning it, trying to put it out. Halcyra lit a torch with a cantrip and the thing recoiled. What it was afraid of was being seen.

When it bit Halcyra, something happened that was worse than the wound: she lost a spell slot. Not to damage. Not to an effect. The creature had reached in and taken a piece of her magic and eaten that too. This is what it was built for. Not to fight. To consume.

Then it grabbed Inquizo in both mouths and ran.

Inquizo, to his credit, fought back. He rolled a 1 and two 3s. He missed absolutely everything. He was in the mouth of a goop creature with two heads and he flailed heroically and achieved nothing, which is a very pure summary of who Inquizo is as a person.

What saved him was George. George ran up to Halcyra mid-combat, touched her on the shoulder, told her she was a hero, and then sang something so beautiful that it healed both her and Inquizo by nine HP each. Then the creature — still carrying Inquizo, still bleeding nightmare substance from where the party had been hitting it, still terrified of the light — made a mistake. It turned its back on Reficul.

Reficul, who was currently a kobold.

The devil’s curse had activated again: fourth time, random form, coin flip result, kobold. And the particular mechanical property of kobolds is that they can walk through other creatures. So Reficul walked through everyone, through the creature, positioned himself directly behind its skull, and launched a mote of radiant light at point-blank range.

Both heads exploded. The body crumpled like a wet bag and turned to goop where it fell, and Inquizo landed on the ground, and the cave was quiet.

---

The aftermath was not clean.

Halcyra and Inquizo both had bite wounds with black goop spreading out from the punctures. Cure Wounds pushed some of it out, but dark shadow marks remained at each entry point — the deep-ache feeling of a shot that hadn’t finished working itself out. Mira collected goop in a bottle and confirmed via salt test that it was chemically identical to the wall writing and the caravan substance. She collected skull shards. She collected the fangs. She grabbed the journal.

The body of the missing merchant was gone. The creature had eaten him completely.

On the walk back, the DM noted that the goop was slowly absorbing into the earth. By the time anyone came looking, there would be nothing there.

At the gate, the Alchemist Guild was still arguing with the caravan about agopyl quantities, and the Enchanter’s Guild was crouched under the wagons with special eyeglasses. George overheard the enchanters before anyone else: they had found runes on the bottom of the wagons. Same alphabet family as the ruins. The same script that Mira had spent a full session researching — the purely magical ruins linked to a deity no one would name. Someone had put that language on the bottom of a merchant caravan from a city that didn’t exist six months ago.

The DM translated the runes, loosely: *sleep. return. catalyst. invite.* And then, in the same sentence: *dinosaur cheesecake.* The language had never been formally deciphered. Things got lost in translation. But the coherent words were coherent enough. Something had been invited. Something had been asked to return.

George said it out loud at the end: *I think there is someone or something making goop constructs in the woods.* Nobody disagreed.

One player, more quietly, asked: *Is this the Nightmare King?*

No one answered that one.

### Session 11: The King's Breakfast

The party split before they ever reached the island city.

Mira and Reficul went through a portal at midnight — a narrow window, operated by a gender-fluid figure who let them through without ceremony. The portal delivered them to a city operating under a regime of fear. The first thing they saw was a body hanging from the portcullis. Above the body, a sign: *Do Not Rebel.* The butcher's corpse swayed. Reficul's curse activated — bullywug this time — and they pressed on.

Meanwhile, Inquisio had gone ahead of everyone and gone invisible.

His recon of the palace was thorough and grim. A prison building held mostly women, birds, and mermaids — the populations this king had decided were threats or tools or both. Bodies on both portcullises. Mert's bedroom: empty. The king was not sleeping. Inquisio tracked what he needed, withdrew, and reported back.

The party gathered. They bought supplies. George bought a war hammer — finally, a real one, even if it wasn't *his*. They disguised Mira (human, relatively unnoticed in a city that surveils the unusual), and they went in.

Their stealth results going through the palace: 28, 24, 14, 8, and George-3. A persuasion roll of 17 got them past the first set of guards. The kitchen was the problem. A chef discovered them. The chef did not survive the discovery. The transition from infiltration to open combat had begun.

What followed in the throne room was one of the biggest fights of the campaign.

King Mert — English breakfast in hand, Nightmare entities as his patron saints, and Nungus's absent blessing behind him — fought the party across two phases. Phase one: conventional king-with-guards combat. He cast Fear; George failed the save and fled the room. He threw his breakfast around with bizarre effectiveness. Reficul hit him with Dissonant Whispers for 32 damage — a single spell, a devastating number. Halcyra applied Searing Smite and lit him on fire. Inquisio pulled the chandelier lever. George came back through the door and used Thunder Wave. Then used it again. The second cast landed in the middle of the wave of guards Mert had summoned — approximately twenty of them — and the room went quiet.

Twenty palace guards died in a single cast. George did not comment on this. He moved on.

Phase two: Mert tapped his shoulder. Earlier in the fight. The party had not known what that meant. He summoned a phantasmal spectator — a CR5 construct of psychic malevolence — and Reficul took it down with Starry Wisp. But the damage was done. They had spent their resources. Mert was near death. And then the contingency activated.

He was gone.

The throne room was quiet. Mert was alive — badly wounded, somewhere he hadn't been a moment ago, and furious. And partially castrated. Because Mira had gotten there with her sickle first.

The pact weapon had manifested during the fight — a sickle, no different in shape from the one Grungrak had given Persephone, but hers, and from a patron she had never consciously agreed to serve. She is an unknowing warlock. Whatever entity is extending her those abilities, it was present in the throne room. After the Zone of Truth interrogation — Mert compelled, briefly, to answer honestly about what he knew — Mira acted with the sickle, and Mert escaped mid-consequence.

He took something with him when he left. Left something behind when he went.

The party stood in a ruined throne room. Twenty dead guards. A spectator's goop. A chandelier on the floor. Nungus already gone north for reasons no one had explained. Mert alive and running.

The DM said: level up to 6.

The session ended there — in the wreckage of a fight that wasn't finished, in a city that was still figuring out whether its king had just been dethroned or merely inconvenienced, in a story that had just grown several new threads.

### Session 12 — The Shadow Selves

The session began in the throne room wreckage and ended on a couch with everyone crying.

The party was still in the island city, still in the ruins of a fight that had escaped them when the king poofed mid-castration. They investigated for Nungus's room and found it pointed to by a neon sign — Vegas-style merch lights all around the throne room, because of course there were — and ended up in the prison. Most of the cells were already open. A tube had been hammered through the glass fish tank at the back, and the mermaids and birds had apparently already left. Only three men remained in the last occupied cell.

One of them had eyes that shifted from brilliant gold to silver. He described being on his farm when blue lightning tore the sky, paper tearing across reality, the sound of a machine. He had been in this prison cell since. Mira did the math and confirmed there was nothing in the historical record about biomatter being merged in the Shattering — but there were whispers about gods appearing differently than they used to, or not answering at all. The farmer's shifting eyes were not his own. Something was layered onto him when the worlds collided. He absorbed this information, said he needed to find someone, and jumped through the fish tank and up the tube into the sea.

Outside on the beach, the party met the Liberators.

They were six men in pastel colors, cheerful and confusing. Their spokesperson was a man named Elton — five foot six, a natural talker, apparently not troubled by the fact that he had arrived in this world by springing from the pages of a book. A seven-foot jovial old man had sent them, they said. Gave them a symbol and told them there were people in danger. This was, the party confirmed to each other in quiet horror, the same figure who had been adjacent to every major mystery in their own journey. The Liberators had already dealt with the birds — they had gone to the eagle king's nest and found a man with wire glasses there, already setting up some kind of system related to sleep. They left satisfied. They had not known what they were looking at.

The party knew.

The wire-glasses man was Nungus. He had been at the Bird Kingdom — at Voxus — two days ago. He was already in position.

The party departed the island city after that. They freed the remaining guards from Mert's chain of command, uncovered the falcon face on the gold-topped bird statue (Mira confirmed it was historically significant; she couldn't name it), and left. The Liberators went separately, toward the fish. The party went north.

Voxus was the Bird Kingdom — built like the Rito from an old story, layered into the mountainside, enormous and structured and ancient in ways the party was not equipped to fully appreciate on a quick visit. They met Gunge. He was an owl-adjacent figure, a shadow therapist, someone who worked with the nightmare side of consciousness as a professional practice. He had something to tell them, but first he had a thing he needed to try.

He walked them through breathing exercises. He faded them to black. And then each of them found themselves somewhere that was not the room they were sitting in.

Mira found herself in a graveyard with a better-dressed version of herself, already doing fine, holding her tethered by something physical. *You could have healed him,* the shadow said. *You don't have the guts.* Mira did not have guts and did not claim to. She had study, and she had intent, and she had the sense that her limits were hers to name. The shadow called this weakness. Mira called it honesty.

Inquizo found it snowing. He could smell someone's breakfast. A voice called his name from a doorway and he ran from it immediately, full sprint, before anyone had finished speaking. His shadow copied every movement effortlessly. The shadow showed him his sister's silhouette in the path ahead and he ran through it. *You fail her again.* He found an alley. His shadow helped him hide. Chains wrapped his arms, his neck. A figure of his brother appeared. The shadow said *we need to protect him* and Inquizo covered what would have been his ears, if he had them, and screamed *what do you want?* The shadow said it wanted what he wanted. He said he had already tried and made things worse. He curled into a ball. The chains became bracelets. He woke up saying *I'm sorry* over and over.

Halcyra was flying — the best flying of her life, sunlight on her wings, utterly free — and an older version of her was beside her asking why she had ever stopped. She said her mother told her to hide. The older Halcyra's face became her mother's and screamed: *Why have you left me?* She crashed into her own farmhouse. Her mother was in front of her. There was also a voice — booming, familiar, kind, coming from the sky and calling her name. The two tugs ran in opposite directions: her mother, begging for one more minute, saying she had something related to her father to give her; and the voice overhead, louder each time, saying *the worlds are at stake.* She went to her mother, whispered *I promise I do love you,* and walked out the door toward the sky.

Reficul was shown a small cottage. Children laughing. His name called lovingly. No contract, no debt, just a life. His shadow had lived it instead of him. *Would you like to hear something about life?* Yes. Everything I can't have. He said the shadow had regrets; the shadow was proud of not making the mistake. He pointed out that the shadow had also never learned anything. *Life is always better when it isn't worse,* he said. The shadow tried arguments about peace and freedom and violence. Reficul kept redirecting. Eventually the shadow said it was looking for a deity who could change form, and Reficul asked *what form are you?* and the shadow said *I am but a shout.* And then it merged with him, becoming a tattoo of darkness across his left arm, because that is the only way to conclude an argument with Reficul — to give up and become part of him.

And then Mira walked into a dragon.

She had been talking to her father's image in the dream when her shadow grew. Green veins became wings. Her head shifted into a dragon's head. Before her stood a blue dragon with green veins — her patron, her dark power, the thing that had been speaking through her in a masculine voice and manifesting the sickle she still barely understood.

Inside the dragon, the DM had expected denial. Mira does not deny; Mira reflects. She walked into it and found Grungrak inside.

The dragon told her she was the one behind the deaths. She asked it what the other Mira — the one in the mirror — had done differently when told to run. *She saved.* Black tendrils came from Mira and held the dragon still. She dealt the final blow. She found the cord — physical, real — and severed it.

And Grungrak became physical. Not a vision. Not a memory. Real. He hugged her and said *thank you for letting me be me* and slowly faded.

The table cried.

After Grungrak faded, her shadow shrank to a small frightened girl. *What am I when they all leave?* You are you. *Am I enough?* What is enough for. Then the girl gave her what she named as her father's final gift — a lightning bolt necklace — and faded.

Mira had learned something. Her father had sold his soul to save her when she was five and first getting sick. The contract read: *my soul for hers.* The patron, the dragon, the masculine voice, the sickle — all of it was downstream of that transaction. She had been purchased. Her father was gone somewhere because of her. And the necklace around her neck was from him, passed through the smallest version of herself, in a dream that was also somehow true.

Gunge, after they woke, gave them tissue boxes and one more piece of information.

He had been on his way to unseal the God of Dreams. Part of the unsealing mechanism was in the next town. The God of Dreams was sealed by her own nightmare — her own shadow — and Nungus was working with that nightmare, for it, deliberately. Gunge believed this had to be stopped. He could not help much. He had duties and a chief who believed the town might need to be destroyed. He would stall as long as he could.

*It's one thing to kill people,* he said. *It's another to torment them in their dreams.*

The party sat on the couch — Inquizo still muttering, George still weeping through his smile, Halcyra holding a bow, Mira holding a necklace, Reficul pretending to be asleep — and understood that the next thing was going to be harder than this one.

## Connecting the Threads

### The Statues

Two god-statues were stolen from the underwater city: "Poppa Poseidon" and the "God of the Seas." The island tribe has at least one — they defaced it and put their leader's face on it. Ariel hired the pirate captain to retrieve them. Ariel is now dead.

This is unresolved. Someone still has the statues. The pirate captain still has a job she was paid for. And two divine artifacts are sitting in the hands of a tribe of men who hate everyone. That does not end quietly.

### The Prophecy and Halcyra

Halcyra doesn't know she's the child of prophecy or Apollo's daughter. Everything about her — the wings, the paladin class, the divine blood — is pointing at a destiny she hasn't been told about. The campaign is three sessions in. The moment she finds out is coming, and when it does, it will change everything about how she understands the last few months of her life.

The underwater city's war with "bird folk" is not a coincidence in this context. Halcyra spent all of Session 2 hiding her wings. The world keeps trying to push her away from her own identity while her identity is quietly the most important thing in the room.

### Mira's Death

Something brought Mira back. The question isn't whether this matters — it clearly does, the field proved that — the question is *who* brought her back and *why*. Options on the table:
- Someone who loves her and didn't want her gone
- Something that needed her for a purpose
- A mistake — she was reinvigorated accidentally, or by a force that didn't fully understand what it was doing

The pit area's white strings may be connected to whatever is anchoring her here, or whatever is trying to pull her back. The fact that she can't heal or pray suggests her connection to divine power is compromised — or that whatever holds her here isn't divine at all.

### Reficul in Hell (and Back, Repeatedly)

Reficul's death mechanic — no death saves, goes to hell — is too specific to be flavor. He has a relationship with the King of Hell. His true form is fiendish. His cause of death in Session 3 was NOT the stab wound and NOT drowning. Something else killed him, and he knew it was coming.

In Session 7, he finally said it plainly: he made a deal with the devil. He cannot die until a job is complete. He cannot have peace until it is done. He is one hundredth done. What the job is, he did not say. A chest of his clothes was already waiting for him inside Persephone's shrine — someone knew he'd be there. His X-salent guild records are damaged, the bottom half gone, including his birthdate. Someone, somewhere, may have ensured these things. That thread has not been pulled yet.

### The Sickle

The sickle "Castrator of the Sky" — Vengeance — was a sentient artifact that Grungrak carried from Session 4 through Session 6. It called itself Vengeance against the sky, against love lost. It caused bloodlust. It had 73% control over Grungrak at the moment he handed it over. Persephone now has it. What a goddess of the underworld intends to do with a weapon forged from a fertility goddess's own body, that craves flesh and whispers in Elvish, is an open question with large implications.

### The Pomegranate

Grungrak ate multiple pomegranate seeds in Persephone's forest. Reficul ate pomegranate. The mythology is explicit — in Greek tradition, eating pomegranate seeds in the underworld binds you there. Persephone herself ate six seeds and is bound to return every year. Grungrak ate more than six. The DM knows the exact count. The character does not know the mythology. This is not resolved. It may not be resolved for several sessions. But it was noted at the table, and it was real.

### The White-Haired Man (Arcus)

He is Arcus — son of Cybele, child of the divine divorce, previously wielded the sickle and descended into Hell. The rose wings, the hyenas, the nails — all of it is part of who he is. He is currently comatose in the Fields of Asphodel after the blood angel's nail-trap triggered a transformation into something large and fluffy with a shaved heart and a green sheep inside. He approached the blood angel with subservience, not aggression, in that form. Something happened to him. The party is carrying him.

### The Jade/Emerald Necklace

Inquizo gave it to Scylla while charmed. The design was a snake eating itself, or possibly a bird — an ouroboros, a symbol of cycles and eternity. Scylla smiled when she took it. Eels do not smile. This was not a random item being taken by a greedy monster. The question is what it was and why Scylla wanted it specifically. The answer has gone underwater with her.

### The Serpent

It has appeared in: the glass mines (seen by Grungrak, who lied about it), Lorya's nail image (staring directly at Inquizo), Arcus's nail image, and on one of Richard's spoons. It keeps appearing. It keeps watching Inquizo specifically. No one has named it yet. It is patient. The necklace Inquizo just gave away depicted a snake eating itself. These things may not be unrelated.

### Halcyra's Prophecy — Still Waiting

Halcyra grew up hearing about a prophecy tied to a chosen hero. She has been searching for that hero for years. In the X-salent tavern, she pulled out a piece of paper. The party was about to read it together when the session ended. In Session 8, she shared it one-on-one with Mira. Session 9, the full party discussion — Prophecy Day — was supposed to happen. The manor happened instead.

Twice now, the prophecy reading has been postponed. This is either the natural chaos of the campaign, or something is actively intervening in the party's ability to sit still long enough to look at the paper. Neither option can be ruled out.

The question remains: does Halcyra understand yet that she is the subject, not the seeker? Does the party? The YOMI letters glowing in Apollo's temple is the most direct divine contact the campaign has shown so far — heat and light from letters she wrote to a god she prays to, confirmed as arcane by Mira. Something is paying attention. Whether that something wants her to read the prophecy, or is trying to get there first, is unknown.

### The Devil's Deals

Reficul cannot die until a job for the devil is complete. He is one hundredth done after everything that has happened. Whatever the job is, it is large. Whatever completion means, it is not just the end of an obligation — Reficul said it means "peace." What does peace mean for someone who cannot die?

### Mira's Patron

When Mira casts death magic, a masculine voice comes out of her instead. When she doesn't stop it, dark ink tendrils shoot from her fingernails and act on their own. It took over her body in combat. It has an agenda. Grungrak's singing is the one thing that gives her back partial control. The patron is real, and Mira is the instrument.

### Ariel's Afterlife — Quietly Closed

The party killed Ariel in Session 3. Eric was her husband — drowned trying to breathe underwater to be with her. He was retrieved from Persephone's soul pool and traveled with the party. In Session 7, Ariel appeared in the River Lethe. She lured Eric in with a stone message. The river took his memories. He forgot her. She forgot him. Ariel swam away not knowing who she was looking for.

This is not a resolution. It is a kind of mercy that hurts more than fighting would have. They were in the same river and they missed each other completely. Whether this thread reopens — whether there's a way to remember, or whether both of them keep searching without knowing why — is unclear. But the "Eric and Ariel" subplot is, for now, quietly over.

### Ariel and Eric — Postscript

The Disney scaffolding was explicit and intentional — a story about a woman who became something terrible out of grief over a lost love. The grief that made Ariel into a tyrant had a person at its center. In the end, that person found her in a river, she gave him a message that said "come here, babe," and the river took everything. Not a villain's death. Just forgetting, in both directions, at exactly the same moment.

### Reficul

Grungrak called out this name in a vision in hell — a face in a puddle of water, a friend he wanted to dive in after. Grungrak does not seem to consciously know who this person is. It may be buried under the amnesia.

### The DO NOT TOUCH Hat

Inquizo cannot read. He put on a hat that says DO NOT TOUCH. The DM flagged it multiple times. It has been on his head for every session since. Whatever this hat does, it is building up.

### The Goop and What It Is

Session 10 collapsed several questions into one.

The black inky substance from the wall writing is the same substance that covered the Velodorn caravan's goods at the gate. It is the same substance the cave creature was made of. Mira confirmed this with salt: all three coagulate identically. They are the same material.

The cave creature was not undead. Not a demon. Not a celestial. It was a construct — something built, deliberately, out of goop. Its most developed feature was a digestive tract. Muscle fibers started at the skull and faded into formlessness toward the extremities. It was built to eat. Consumption was its primary and possibly only function.

It was also invisible before it fed, and it hated light. These are not the properties of a natural predator. These are design specifications.

Someone built it. Someone put runes from the ruins alphabet on the bottom of a Velodorn merchant’s wagons. Velodorn appeared five months ago, when the world broke. The ruins are linked to a deity the academic literature refuses to name. The wall writing in X-salent’s wealthy district is the same script. The Victorian manor district still has possessed houses. Halcyra and Inquizo have dark shadow marks in their bite wounds that didn’t fully heal. And Mira has the dead merchant’s journal.

The rune translation said: *sleep. return. catalyst. invite.*

Something was invited. Something was asked to return. The party killed one construct. The source is still out there, and the caravan campsite in the woods hasn’t been investigated yet.

### The Mysterious Figure

Reficul spotted someone running from the direction of the Guild Hall through slum alleys. He tracked them. He sent a cryptic message: *"Divinity needs your haste. Meet the tavern in two hours."* He fought them. They teleported away.

They received the message. They are, presumably, capable of being reached by it — which means they are real, they are paying attention, and they made a choice. Whether that choice was to show up at the tavern or to disappear further into the city is unresolved. But someone out there knows Reficul tried to contact them, and they know when.

What were they running from? Why from the direction of the Guild Hall? Were they at the scene of the writing before the party arrived?

### George and Mira — Thread Closed, Story Continuing

The tension between Grungrak/George and Mira — built up over multiple sessions of half-truths about Reficul's deaths, avoidance dressed as kindness, and grief that neither of them had named out loud — resolved in Session 8 in a street in the middle of X-salent. He apologized. She accepted. He told her he had met Grungrak in the Lethe and found him kind. She did not have to pretend anymore that she did not know him from before.

The thread is closed. The friendship is not — it is continuing, differently, with both of them slightly less alone than they were before. George asked Mira to tell him the tale of a jolly good fellow. That story is still coming.

### Mira's Patron — Now Named

The patron is a dragon. A blue dragon with green veins — Mira's own veins. It appeared in the shadow therapy session when her shadow grew past its human shape into something massive. Grungrak was inside it. The dragon claimed to be the one in power, the one behind the deaths, the one who had always been there.

And then Mira walked into it.

The origin is now established: her father sold his soul to the dragon when Mira was five years old and first getting sick. The contract terms were *my soul for hers.* The masculine voice that speaks through her during death magic, the sickle that manifested in the throne room, the dark ink tendrils from her fingernails — all of it flows from that contract. She did not agree to this. She was five. Her father loved her and made a terrible and irreversible decision.

The sickle is still notable for what it echoes: the "Castrator of the Sky" — Vengeance — that Grungrak carried and Persephone now holds. Whether Mira's pact weapon and that artifact share a lineage is unresolved. But two sickles, one campaign.

## Session 13 — The Inheritance of Strangers

They woke on Gunge's couch still raw from the night before. The items were on their bodies. The shadows were still gone — or compressed, or merged, or waiting. The session began with the weight of what had just happened still sitting in the room, and then Cooper said "okay, you're awake" and the world asked them to get up and keep going.

The first revelation came from Valu. Not the Valu anyone had anticipated — not a solemn wind god, but a flighty, distracted presence who handed out sending stones like an afterthought and then wasn't there anymore. He's the one inside the Thorin statue. He manifested just long enough for the party to understand the mechanic: bring a statue to its people, and the god inside can breathe. He confirmed the pattern without explaining the cost.

They took Mert's airship south — back to the island city they'd liberated and then left, which had done what liberated places sometimes do when the liberators don't stay. The people had seized power, and power in a city that had known nothing but brutality for years looked exactly like what it was: unfiltered, unsupervised, and dangerous. The guards were in the cells. The loudest man in town had built a platform overnight and started scheduling who would die next.

Reficul walked up to the platform when the crowd chanted his name. He was handed an axe and asked to do what he was supposedly known for — confirm the new regime's righteousness through violence. He didn't take the axe and step back. He took the axe, walked to the edge of the platform, and gave a speech about death. He said death is not punishment; death is freedom; pain is the actual sentence; make a person live with what they've done and make them change. And then someone in the crowd called out to get the women and children — and Reficul threw the axe into that person and split them in half.

The crowd fled. Naamon confronted him. Reficul punched him. Mira caught the body. Naamon was dead from the punch — a normal person, not built to survive a seven-foot warlock with two deaths and hell on his resume.

Mira said, holding the dead man in her arms: *This is not the first time I have witnessed you kill someone because you were angry.*

Reficul did not argue. He said: *I don't feel good about this.*

And then he said he was staying. He couldn't leave again. He had left before and this is what it looked like. He would stay and help them — however long it took — to find their footing. The party said goodbye in the way you say goodbye when you don't know when you'll see someone again and the world is already moving fast. George told him don't be scared of the stick, and if you have to change yourself to sleep, that's okay. Reficul said he was not going to go on vacation somewhere this time. They both meant something else by both of those sentences.

The Thorin statue wept. One tear. No glow, no light, just a single tear from a carved eye falling to the pavement. The statue was not in the right place. Neither was the party. They loaded it onto the ship.

George had been caught, in the meantime, singing quietly to himself — a song about Mira that he'd been practicing in secret for eight sessions, a ballad someone had told him about a long time ago in a world he didn't entirely remember yet. He was caught. He ran. He spent an hour pacing the throne room trying to calm himself down and failing once. By the time he showed up to help move the statue he'd gotten it mostly under control, but his face was still deeply suspicious. Mira told him she wouldn't say anything. He did not seem convinced.

They went north.

On the airship they found the name first — "Starglass" — on a nameplate that only revealed itself when a lever was pulled. Then a map to Sypium, hidden in a compartment. Then dossiers with faces and essays, including one about a person they hadn't met yet. Then Inquizo bumped a button while wearing Mert's crown and the ship's hologram AI greeted King Mert, and Inquizo mimicked Mert's voice and said "I want to go to Sypium" and the ship started going north, which is where they wanted to go anyway. And then Halcyra found the grandfather clock, and behind the grandfather clock, behind the wallpaper, behind everything, she found Starglass.

Starglass is a person. Or was a person. She is now what she is: fleshy and mechanical and connected to the ship by pipes that breathe air through her body so the ship can move. Bugs live in her eyes. When she is emotional, the ship changes. She has been here since the Doctor put her here, since Mert gave his crown in exchange for this ship, since whatever happened at her mother's home that she can only partially remember.

Her mother is Malafera. The rune alphabet — the circles-and-clouds writing that has appeared on walls and wagons and underground murals since Session 8 — is her mother's language. The goop. The constructs. The things made of dark matter that consume and move and build themselves. All of this traces back to Malafera, who is missing, who is taken or hiding or gone. Her brother Haki went north to protect a girl who glows and is universally known and Starglass was never told her name. The danger is north. She pointed north when they asked.

And underneath a tapestry showing a sun and wings and a flock of sheep, there is another glass case. Foggy. Faint blue. Halcyra tripped on the tapestry's edge and found it. Nobody opened it. The ship is rebooting.

They are heading north.
