# Larkin's Observational Report — Allen Prothero

*Luminary of Mechanical Regulation, Royal Council of Aerolon. Year 1718.*

---

**Subject:** Allen Prothero
**Occupation:** Chief Structural Engineer, Independent Consultant to the Council
**Affiliation:** Public Works Initiative, Private Contracting Networks

---

Allen Prothero represents a rare convergence of aesthetic philosophy and mechanical genius.

Where most engineers of Aerolon concern themselves with efficiency, output, and scalability, Prothero's work demonstrates an unusual preoccupation with **integration** — specifically, the seamless blending of infrastructure into the natural environment. His recent innovations in cable architecture have begun to fundamentally alter the visual and functional identity of several districts within the capital and its surrounding commonwealths.

Traditional electrical distribution systems, characterized by large conductive towers and exposed lines, are being systematically replaced under his guidance with **subdermal cabling networks**. These systems are threaded beneath streets, woven through existing architecture, and in some cases grown alongside living plant structures in Lozenlind's experimental districts.

The result is twofold:
- **Aesthetic Preservation:** Cityscapes remain visually unobstructed.
- **Functional Efficiency:** Energy loss across distances is reduced significantly through his insulated pressure-conductive filaments.

---

Of particular note is Prothero's development of **flex-conductive lattice wiring** — a material capable of carrying both electrical current and pressurized air simultaneously. This dual-function design has enabled the creation of hybrid transport systems, wherein gondolas and railcars draw power directly from the infrastructure beneath them without reliance on visible overhead systems.

Public reception has been overwhelmingly positive.

However, several concerns warrant continued observation.

Prothero's methods rely heavily on **decentralization**. By embedding critical infrastructure throughout the environment rather than consolidating it into centralized hubs, he has created a system that is both resilient and difficult to fully control. In the event of sabotage or systemic failure, isolating the source of disruption would prove considerably more complex than with traditional designs.

Additionally, his collaboration with botanical researchers in Lozenlind has produced early-stage prototypes of **bio-reactive wiring** — capable of adapting to environmental stimuli. While promising, such systems introduce variables that are not yet fully understood.

---

It is my professional assessment that Prothero is not driven by political ambition, but rather by a genuine belief that technology should serve as an extension of nature rather than its replacement.

This belief is admirable.

Should he become a rogue, it is also potentially dangerous.

Systems that blend too well into their surroundings become difficult to see.

Systems that cannot be seen are difficult to regulate.

---

Nevertheless, his influence on the next generation of engineers is undeniable. His current apprentice, **Fletcher Kardos**, has already begun demonstrating similar tendencies in design philosophy.

If Prothero's work continues unimpeded, Aerolon may soon become the first civilization to achieve true infrastructural invisibility.

Whether such a development represents progress or vulnerability remains to be determined.

*— Dr. Alistair Larkin*
*Year 1718*
